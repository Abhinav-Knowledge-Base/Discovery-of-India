# Ep 86 — Gauda Under Shashanka

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Shashanka makes Gauda a major eastern power, though hostile rivals and sparse records leave his career contested. |
| **Years** | c. 590–638 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-Gupta eastern polities → Gauda’s brief ascendancy and renewed regional competition |

## Before
Bengal’s delta, Rarh uplands, ports, forests, and river systems supported several political zones rather than a timeless unified Bengal.

## The turn
Shashanka establishes Karnasuvarna, contests the Maukharis and Harsha, engages Kamarupa, and issues Shaiva royal claims.

## After
His realm fragments after his death; later Pala memory develops amid very different political conditions.

## Act I — Building Gauda
Distinguish Gauda, Vanga, Samatata, and other geographic names whose meanings shift across sources.

## Act II — A rival’s villain
Compare Bana’s and Xuanzang’s accusations with contemporary inscriptions, refusing either acquittal or conviction beyond evidence.

## Act III — Karnasuvarna
Use Rajbaridanga archaeology to connect court, monastery, settlement, and river routes.

## Act IV — Delta and frontier
Follow agrarian expansion and grants alongside fishing, boating, forest production, and communities poorly seen in elite records.

## Act V — A short ascendancy
End with succession uncertainty and multiple eastern powers, not an inevitable march toward later Bengali statehood.

## Subcontinental note
Gauda interacted with Kamarupa, Odisha, Nepal routes, Magadha, and Bay of Bengal networks without encompassing the Northeast or eastern coast.

## How we know
Seals, copper plates, coins, archaeology, Bana, and Xuanzang survive unevenly; hostile narrative sources postdate key events.

## Do not claim
- Do not state as fact that Shashanka cut the Bodhi tree.
- Do not map Gauda as identical to modern Bengal.
- Do not define Shashanka solely through Harsha’s perspective.
- Do not assume Shaiva patronage meant systematic Buddhist persecution.
- Do not erase delta and forest communities from agrarian expansion.
