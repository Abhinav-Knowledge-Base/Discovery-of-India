# Ep 73 — The Guptas Begin

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | From a modest lineage in the middle Ganga plain, the Guptas build a court whose coins and marriage claims advertise expanding power. |
| **Years** | c. 275–335 CE |
| **Before–after** | Kushan and Satavahana worlds → Chandragupta I and an emergent Gupta realm |

## Before
North India was divided among Kushan successors, Nagas, forest polities, gana-sanghas, and regional courts linked by trade and pilgrimage.

## The turn
Gupta, Ghatotkacha, and Chandragupta I move from terse genealogies to a royal language of *maharajadhiraja*, alliance, and conquest.

## After
Samudragupta inherits a compact but ambitious base; later praise will make its beginnings appear more inevitable than they were.

## Act I — After empires
Map post-Kushan corridors and Ganga settlements without presenting political fragmentation as civilizational decline.

## Act II — Names in a genealogy
Read the Allahabad pillar genealogy backward, stressing that dynastic memory is selective and composed under later success.

## Act III — A marriage on gold
The Chandragupta–Kumaradevi coin links Gupta and Lichchhavi prestige, but cannot by itself prove a dowry of territory.

## Act IV — Power and countryside
Set court claims beside agrarian expansion, craft production, forest communities, and uneven evidence for caste and gender relations.

## Act V — An unfinished beginning
End before Samudragupta’s campaigns, with multiple kingdoms and routes still shaping the subcontinent.

## Subcontinental note
The Guptas were one power among many: Vakatakas, Pallavas, Sri Lankan kingdoms, Himalayan communities, and western ports had distinct histories and connections.

## How we know
Later inscriptions, coin sequences, seals, archaeology, and Puranic lists are unevenly dated; none offers a neutral year-by-year narrative.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim a precise Gupta “founding year” from uncertain era calculations.
- Do not claim the Lichchhavi marriage automatically transferred a kingdom.
- Do not claim Gupta rise ended trade or forest autonomy everywhere.
- Do not claim caste rules in normative texts describe all lived communities.
- Do not claim a single “classical India” began with one dynasty.

## Figures, sites, and works

| Gupta–Lichchhavi coin | Visual claim about alliance, legitimacy, and gendered sovereignty |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Prayagraj’s pillar context, middle-Ganga landscapes, coin obverse and reverse under raking light, and excavated settlement layers; label every reconstructed boundary as approximate.
