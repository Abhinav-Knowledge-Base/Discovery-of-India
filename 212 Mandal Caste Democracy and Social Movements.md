## Before

Constitutional reservations covered Scheduled Castes and Tribes, but Other Backward Classes had uneven access to national representation.

## The turn

The Mandal Commission and 1990 implementation made backward-class reservation a national political fault line amid protests and judicial review.

## After

OBC leadership and coalition politics expanded, while Dalit, Adivasi, women's, environmental, farmer, and queer movements pressed distinct agendas.

## Act I - Mandal's evidence

The commission used imperfect data to identify social and educational backwardness and recommend central reservations.

## Act II - Protest and adjudication

Student self-immolations, support mobilizations, and Indra Sawhney shaped the 27 percent policy and legal ceiling.

## Act III - Regional transformations

Dravidian, socialist, Bahujan, and agrarian parties built different caste coalitions and welfare programs.

## Act IV - Dalit assertion

The Dalit Panthers, BAMCEF, BSP, literature, and atrocity protests challenged both upper-caste power and OBC dominance.

## Act V - Movement democracy

Chipko, Narmada, autonomous women's groups, labor, disability, and sexuality campaigns expanded rights vocabularies.

## Subcontinental note

Mandal politics differed in north and south; Northeast tribal representation, Kashmir, and state-specific lists complicate any national caste ladder.

## How we know

Commission reports, court judgments, election surveys, party documents, protest posters, movement writing, oral histories, and atrocity data reveal both gains and violence.

## Do not claim

- Mandal created caste consciousness from nothing.
- All OBC or Dalit interests are identical.
- Reservation is the whole of social justice.
- Social movements stood outside electoral and state politics.
