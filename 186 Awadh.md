## Before

Mughal provincial government depended on taluqdars, soldiers, bankers, cultivators, and Lucknow's networks.

## The turn

Nawabs built autonomy while Company loans, troops, and treaties progressively redirected finances and diplomacy.

## After

Annexation displaced court and landed interests, intensified grievances, and made Awadh central to the 1857 war.

## Act I - Regional court

Saadat Khan's line combined imperial office with revenue and patronage.

## Act II - Company connection

Buxar and treaties made Awadh buffer, debtor, ally, and recruiting ground.

## Act III - Lucknow publics

Poetry, Shia institutions, crafts, courtesans, and shared patronage made a complex city.

## Act IV - Countryside

Revenue moved through taluqdars while peasants negotiated, fled, litigated, and resisted.

## Act V - Annexation argument

Officials used selective misrule claims to absorb an already constrained state.

## Subcontinental note

Awadh linked Lucknow and Faizabad to Banaras, Delhi, Nepal, and cantonments through pilgrimage, credit, labor, and recruitment.

## How we know

Treaties, Urdu and Persian texts, surveys, art, petitions, buildings, and rebel proclamations preserve opposed views.

## Do not claim

- Annexation reflected neutral humanitarian concern.
- Court culture was merely decadent.
- Taluqdars and peasants had identical interests.
- Awadh represents every 1857 region.
