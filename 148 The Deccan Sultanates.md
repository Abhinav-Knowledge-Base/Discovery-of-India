## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Five successor courts compete, intermarry, trade, and patronize new Deccani forms before Mughal and Maratha expansion redraws power. |
| **Years** | c. 1490–1687 |
| **Before–after** | From Bahmani fragmentation to Mughal annexation and resilient regional cultures. |
## Before
- Bahmani governors command provinces, forts, revenue networks, and military clients.
- The plateau already links inland production to Arabian Sea ports.
## The turn
- Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Berar, Bidar, and Golconda assert sovereign titles.
- Competition produces shifting alliances rather than fixed confessional camps.
## After
- Ahmadnagar resists through Malik Ambar before Mughal absorption.
- Bijapur and Golconda fall in 1686–87, but their institutions outlive dynasties.
## Act I — Five courts
- Introduce Nizam Shahi, Adil Shahi, Imad Shahi, Barid Shahi, and Qutb Shahi houses.
- Track forts and tribute zones rather than neat modern borders.
- Dynasties draw on Persian, Turkic, African, Maratha, Telugu, and Kannada talent.
## Act II — Talikota reconsidered
- In 1565 several sultanates ally against Vijayanagara for strategic reasons.
- Rama Raya's earlier diplomacy crossed the same political field.
- Defeat and plunder were real; “civilizational clash” is inadequate.
## Act III — Courts of experiment
- Ibrahim Adil Shah II's *Kitab-i Nauras* joins music, devotion, and kingship.
- Golconda's Telugu and Persian patronage serves a multilingual realm.
- Shared aesthetics never cancel military extraction or court hierarchy.
## Act IV — Diamonds, ports, and labor
- Golconda connects mines, Coromandel textiles, Masulipatnam, and global merchants.
- Revenue farms and artisanal labor underpin elite splendor.
- Habshi commanders and Malik Ambar demonstrate African agency in Deccan politics.
## Act V — Imperial pressure
- Mughal campaigns exploit succession disputes but meet difficult terrain and resistance.
- Maratha lineages negotiate service, land rights, and autonomous power.
- Annexation expands Mughal claims without producing uniform control.
## Subcontinental note
- The Deccan is a political center with its own oceanic and inland worlds, not a northern periphery.
- Sunni, Shia, Hindu, and other affiliations shape action without mechanically determining it.
## How we know
- Persian chronicles are court-centered and often hostile to rivals.
- Paintings, music texts, coins, inscriptions, and architecture expose alternative priorities.
- European factory records illuminate trade while filtering society through commerce.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim the five states acted as one Muslim bloc.
- Do not claim Talikota alone destroyed Vijayanagara society.
- Do not claim “composite culture” made coercion disappear.
- Do not claim Mughal conquest ended Deccani agency.
