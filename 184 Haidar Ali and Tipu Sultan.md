## Before

Mysore already possessed revenue institutions, military labor markets, and a strategic capital when Haidar rose through command.

## The turn

The rulers centralized resources, expanded armies and commerce, and fought coalitions linking the Company, Marathas, and Hyderabad.

## After

Tipu's defeat enabled Company paramountcy in the south, a dependent Wodeyar restoration, and territorial redistribution.

## Act I - Haidar's ascent

Court faction, military success, and fiscal command made Haidar effective ruler.

## Act II - War and diplomacy

Four wars were coalition conflicts in which Indian alliances changed.

## Act III - Tipu's statecraft

Monopolies, missions, coinage, rockets, and administration sought strength.

## Act IV - Subjects and coercion

Malabar and Kodagu experienced taxes, opportunity, deportation, and violence.

## Act V - Srirangapatna 1799

Company forces prevailed with allied Indian armies and resources.

## Subcontinental note

Conflicts joined Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil regions, the Deccan, Mauritius, the Gulf, and French revolutionary diplomacy.

## How we know

Mysorean orders, Company letters, chronicles, religious records, weapons, coins, and archaeology require source criticism.

## Do not claim

- Tipu was simply a secular nationalist or religious tyrant.
- Every policy across Mysore was uniform.
- Rockets alone explain Mysore's power.
- British victory was inevitable.
