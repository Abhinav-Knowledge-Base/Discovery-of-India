## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Pilgrims, performers, temples, and Marathi verse make Pandharpur a devotional network shaped by caste, labor, and patronage. |
| **Years** | c. thirteenth–seventeenth centuries |
| **Before–after** | From regional Vitthal worship to a durable Varkari pilgrimage public. |
## Before
- Pandharpur stands within Yadava, pastoral, agrarian, and temple networks.
- Vitthal's origins remain debated and resist one genealogy.
## The turn
- Jnaneshwar and Namdev become anchors of Marathi devotional memory.
- Pilgrimage and sung abhangs connect dispersed households to Pandharpur.
## After
- Eknath and Tukaram renew textual and ethical debate.
- Palkhi institutions later regularize routes and saint lineages.
## Act I — Deity and town
- Read Vitthal's standing image through inscription, iconography, and living theology.
- Temple service requires priests, donors, vendors, musicians, and laborers.
- Access remains structured by caste and authority.
## Act II — Marathi scripture
- Jnaneshwar's *Jnaneshwari* renders the *Gita* through vernacular commentary.
- Namdev traditions travel across Marathi and northern manuscript worlds.
- Attributions shift as communities build canons.
## Act III — Walking devotion
- The wari makes roads, halts, food, song, and discipline into institutions.
- Pilgrims differ by caste, gender, wealth, and bodily ability.
- Shared walking does not automatically equal social equality.
## Act IV — Marginalized voices
- Chokhamela poems confront untouchability from devotional belonging.
- Janabai makes domestic labor and intimacy speak.
- Canonization can celebrate pain without changing exclusion.
## Act V — Tukaram's public
- Tukaram criticizes status, hypocrisy, debt, and hollow learning.
- Manuscript and oral transmission complicate the story of drowned verses.
- Later movements repeatedly remake him for political needs.
## Subcontinental note
- Varkari practice intersects Nath, Brahmanical, Sufi, and mercantile worlds without collapsing into them.
- Marathi devotion has institutions and conflicts, not poetry alone.
## How we know
- Inscriptions anchor temple patronage and some early names.
- Abhang anthologies are late, variant, and performative.
- Hagiographies, routes, objects, and oral repertoires reveal changing memory.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim the wari erased caste.
- Do not claim every attributed abhang is authorial.
- Do not claim Vitthal has one proven origin.
- Do not treat today's palkhi organization as unchanged medieval practice.
