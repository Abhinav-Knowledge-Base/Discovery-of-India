## Before

Communities used varied commons, shifting cultivation, grazing, water, and subsistence strategies alongside regional markets.

## The turn

Revenue rigidity, export crops, forest reservation, plantation discipline, and indenture expanded under coercive legal regimes.

## After

Millions died or migrated, while resistance, ecological change, labor cultures, and diasporas reshaped India and the wider ocean world.

## Act I - Famine as policy crisis

Drought met purchasing-power loss, grain trade, weak relief, and official ideology in different famines.

## Act II - Reserved forests

The state restricted grazing, fuel, shifting cultivation, and hunting while extracting timber for railways.

## Act III - Plantation regimes

Tea, coffee, indigo, and cinchona used recruitment debt, penal contracts, and gendered supervision.

## Act IV - Indentured oceans

Workers crossed to Mauritius, the Caribbean, Fiji, Africa, and Malaya under contracts after slavery's abolition.

## Act V - Survival and resistance

Flight, food claims, forest offenses, strikes, songs, gardens, and kinship challenged imposed systems.

## Subcontinental note

Madras, Deccan, Bengal, central forests, Assam, Bihar, and overseas colonies experienced distinct ecologies; diaspora history remains subcontinental history.

## How we know

Famine codes, price data, mortality estimates, forest files, plantation registers, ship lists, photographs, songs, and descendants' testimony have different silences.

## Do not claim

- Drought alone caused colonial famines.
- Forests were empty wilderness.
- Indenture was either free migration or identical to slavery.
- Women experienced plantation and migration like men.
