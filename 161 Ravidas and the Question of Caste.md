## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Ravidas turns stigmatized leather work and caste exclusion into a theology of dignity whose communities reshape his memory. |
| **Years** | c. fifteenth–seventeenth centuries, with modern afterlives |
| **Before–after** | From Banaras devotional networks to Ravidasi canons, shrines, and political publics. |
## Before
- Leather-working communities face untouchability while sustaining essential urban economies.
- Sant circles overlap without forming one egalitarian institution.
## The turn
- Poems attributed to Ravidas condemn caste pride and imagine Begampura, a city without sorrow or tax.
- His occupational identity becomes central rather than incidental to reception.
## After
- Sikh scripture preserves forty compositions under Ravidas's name.
- Ravidasi institutions create autonomous authority amid continuing caste violence.
## Act I — Labor and stigma
- Show tanning and leather work with worker consent and no aestheticized degradation.
- Purity rules turn indispensable labor into inherited exclusion.
- Ravidas's “chamar” self-naming bears social and theological force.
## Act II — Begampura
- Read the poem as utopian critique of tax, fear, ownership, and rank.
- Do not reduce its vocabulary to a modern policy blueprint.
- Performance links hope to experiences of displacement and humiliation.
## Act III — Textual lives
- The *Adi Granth* offers an early bounded corpus.
- North Indian manuscripts and oral songs preserve variants and additions.
- Hagiographies linking Mirabai express devotional genealogy, not certain encounter.
## Act IV — Patronage and gender
- Elite devotees may honor Ravidas while caste structures persist.
- Women's participation appears in song and hagiography but is underdocumented.
- Shrines, deras, and gurus require property, donations, and management.
## Act V — Community afterlives
- Colonial enumeration hardens some caste labels while enabling new associations.
- Twentieth-century Ravidasi assertion claims text, place, and representation.
- Treat current identity as historical agency, not proof of an unchanged sect.
## Subcontinental note
- Caste crosses religious communities in different forms and cannot be assigned to one tradition alone.
- Anti-caste devotion must be judged alongside institutions and material access.
## How we know
- Scriptural compositions are stronger evidence than late biographies, but still transmitted texts.
- Caste histories require oral testimony, administrative records, and material sites.
- Modern institutions illuminate reception, not direct medieval continuity.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Ravidas's movement abolished caste.
- Do not claim Begampura describes an actual medieval city.
- Do not claim Mirabai's discipleship is established biography.
- Do not use “low caste” without naming imposed hierarchy.
