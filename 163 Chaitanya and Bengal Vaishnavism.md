## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Chaitanya's ecstatic Krishna devotion becomes a learned, landed, and performative Gaudiya Vaishnava institution. |
| **Years** | 1486–c. 1650 |
| **Before–after** | From Nadia's scholastic world to networks linking Bengal, Puri, and Vrindavan. |
## Before
- Bengal already sustains Krishna poetry, temple worship, tantra, and diverse Vaishnava lineages.
- Sultanate patronage and Brahmanical institutions coexist in unequal local settings.
## The turn
- Chaitanya makes congregational kirtan and Radha-Krishna love central to a charismatic following.
- Renunciation and residence at Puri transform regional devotion into a mobile network.
## After
- Vrindavan Goswamis systematize theology, pilgrimage geography, and practice.
- Bengali lineages, temples, manuscripts, and festivals institutionalize memory.
## Act I — Nabadwip
- Place Chaitanya within Sanskrit learning, household status, and urban competition.
- Early kirtan contests public space and authority.
- Hagiographies heighten confrontation and miracle for devotional purposes.
## Act II — Ecstasy and discipline
- Dance, tears, song, and bodily transformation authorize devotion.
- Collective performance requires leaders, musicians, households, and funding.
- Charisma does not remove rules or hierarchy.
## Act III — Puri
- Jagannath's temple and Gajapati court frame Chaitanya's later life.
- Temple access remains caste-regulated despite universal devotional claims.
- Stories of Prataparudra dramatize negotiation between renouncer and king.
## Act IV — Vrindavan remade
- Rupa, Sanatana, and Jiva Goswami define sacred sites and textual theology.
- Mughal-era patronage and Rajput support enable temples and landholding.
- Recovered geography combines memory, scripture, and new construction.
## Act V — Caste and gender
- Nityananda and other lineages broaden participation unevenly.
- Women such as Jahnava Devi exercise leadership despite male-centered records.
- Dalit and Muslim-associated devotees are celebrated within persistent boundaries.
## Subcontinental note
- Gaudiya Vaishnavism develops inside Bengal Sultanate and Mughal political worlds, not outside them.
- Its Krishna devotion is one Vaishnava formation, not all Hinduism.
## How we know
- Early hagiographies disagree and pursue theological agendas.
- Sanskrit treatises, Bengali songs, land grants, temple remains, and manuscripts diversify evidence.
- Pilgrimage tradition may preserve memory while relocating events.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Chaitanya invented Bengali Krishna devotion.
- Do not claim kirtan erased caste and gender hierarchy.
- Do not claim every hagiographic miracle is biography.
- Do not claim court patronage made the movement politically uniform.
