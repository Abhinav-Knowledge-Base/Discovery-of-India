## Before

Mobile growth, rights-based welfare laws, local health systems, informal labor, and uneven internet access shaped the digital transition.

## The turn

Aadhaar-linked services, direct transfers, UPI, platform work, and data governance expanded before COVID-19 tested institutions at exceptional scale.

## After

Vaccination and digital services achieved large reach, while lockdown migration, learning loss, mortality measurement, and surveillance remained contested.

## Act I - Digital public infrastructure

Identity, bank accounts, mobile systems, and payments enabled scale but authentication failure and exclusion required safeguards.

## Act II - Platform economy

Delivery, ride-hailing, e-commerce, and remote work created convenience and income alongside algorithmic control and insecure labor.

## Act III - Lockdown and migration

The 2020 national restrictions sharply limited movement; migrant workers faced lost wages, transport crises, and long journeys.

## Act IV - Public health response

Federal responsibilities, laboratories, oxygen, hospitals, vaccines, community workers, and scientific debate changed across waves.

## Act V - Evidence and accountability

Civil registration, excess-mortality studies, dashboards, court hearings, journalism, and household surveys produced disputed estimates.

## Subcontinental note

Kerala, Maharashtra, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, the Northeast, rural districts, and migrant corridors had different capacities and outcomes; states were active actors.

## How we know

Statutes, Supreme Court rulings, audit reports, platform data, health bulletins, genomic studies, surveys, worker testimony, and mortality series have limits.

## Do not claim

- Digital delivery eliminates exclusion by definition.
- The pandemic had one uniform Indian experience.
- Official and excess-mortality figures measure the same thing.
- Criticism or praise of policy substitutes for institutional evidence.
