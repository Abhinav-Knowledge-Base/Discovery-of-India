# Ep 85 — Harsha and Kannauj

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Harsha builds a northern coalition from Kannauj, while court poetry and a pilgrim’s memoir magnify a realm bounded by rivals. |
| **Years** | c. 606–647 CE |
| **Before–after** | Pushyabhuti and Maukhari crisis → Harsha’s paramountcy and rapid posthumous fragmentation |

## Before
Maukharis, Gauda, Malwa powers, later Guptas, Chalukyas, and regional elites competed after Gupta contraction.

## The turn
Dynastic murders and alliances bring Harsha to power; campaigns, assemblies, gifts, and diplomacy make Kannauj a prestigious center.

## After
No secure heir preserves his configuration, and Kannauj becomes a prize among later regional powers.

## Act I — A family catastrophe
Reconstruct the succession through Bana’s literary *Harshacharita*, marking romance, convention, and missing voices.

## Act II — Making Kannauj
Follow river routes, camps, officials, monasteries, temples, villages, and tributary rulers behind a mobile court.

## Act III — The Narmada limit
Pulakeshin II’s record checks claims of universal conquest and places the Chalukya Deccan on equal narrative ground.

## Act IV — Xuanzang’s journey
Use the pilgrim’s observations for institutions and routes while questioning numbers, retrospective editing, and Buddhist priorities.

## Act V — Patronage and performance
Harsha’s plays, religious giving, and assemblies suggest plurality, not a clean conversion from Shaivism to Buddhism.

## Subcontinental note
Kamarupa, Gauda, Nepal, Kashmir, Sindh, Valabhi, and the Deccan appear as connected powers, not provinces waiting to join Kannauj.

## How we know
Bana, Xuanzang, inscriptions, seals, coins, and Chalukya praise texts are partisan and leave administration and chronology uneven.

## Do not claim
- Do not call Harsha emperor of the entire subcontinent.
- Do not narrate Bana’s courtly episodes as verbatim fact.
- Do not claim Xuanzang counted populations with modern precision.
- Do not present Harsha’s religious identity as exclusive or static.
- Do not infer universal welfare from spectacular royal donations.
