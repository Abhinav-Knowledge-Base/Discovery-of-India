# Ep 95 — Tibet, Nepal, and the Himalayan Roads

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Tibetan and Licchavi courts, Newar artisans, monks, traders, and mountain communities turn Himalayan roads into political and sacred networks. |
| **Years** | c. 500–850 CE |
| **Before–after** | Early Himalayan polities → Tibetan imperial expansion and connected Nepal Valley courts |

## Before
The Himalaya contained valleys, high pastures, forest zones, salt and wool routes, pilgrimage places, and communities never governed as one region.

## The turn
Licchavi inscriptions illuminate Nepal; the Tibetan empire expands across the plateau and contests Central Asian and Himalayan corridors.

## After
Tibetan imperial fragmentation and Nepalese political changes alter routes, while Buddhist and artisanal exchanges continue.

## Act I — Roads, not barriers
Follow seasonal passes, pack animals, river valleys, and climatic risk to replace the image of an impassable wall.

## Act II — Licchavi Nepal
Read Changu Narayan and later inscriptions for rulers, taxes, waterworks, caste claims, and settlements while centering Newar agency.

## Act III — The Tibetan empire
Use Old Tibetan Annals and archaeology to frame Songtsen Gampo and successors beyond later Buddhist hagiography.

## Act IV — Translating Buddhism
Monks, patrons, translators, and artisans move texts and images through Kashmir, Nepal, northern India, and Tibet in multiple directions.

## Act V — Border communities
Bring women in dynastic diplomacy, pastoralists, traders, forest users, and local deities into a story dominated by courts.

## Subcontinental note
Nepal and Tibet are neighboring historical centers connected to South Asia, China, and Central Asia; they are not northern extensions of India.

## How we know
Stone inscriptions, Old Tibetan documents, Chinese chronicles, art, archaeology, and later religious biographies preserve incompatible chronologies and agendas.

## Do not claim
- Do not portray the Himalaya as an empty barrier or timeless border.
- Do not reduce Tibetan Buddhism to passive import from India.
- Do not call Licchavi Nepal a province of any plains empire.
- Do not treat royal marriage legends as uncontested fact.
- Do not assign modern national identities to mobile early medieval communities.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Songtsen Gampo | Tibetan ruler later surrounded by religious memory |
| Bhrikuti | Nepalese princess figure whose historicity is debated |
| Amshuverma | Powerful Nepal Valley ruler known from inscriptions |
| Changu Narayan | Early dated inscription and sacred site |
| Samye | Early Tibetan monastery and translation landmark |
| Old Tibetan Annals | Near-contemporary record distinct from later chronicles |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Kathmandu Valley inscriptions and waterworks, high passes, caravan routes, Samye, manuscripts, and metalwork; map seasons and elevation, not just borders.
