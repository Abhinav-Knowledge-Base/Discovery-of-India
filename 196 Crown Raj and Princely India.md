## Before

Company territories and hundreds of treaty states already formed a layered political map linked by residents, armies, and tribute.

## The turn

The 1858 transfer promised noninterference and princely security while viceroys, civil servants, and paramountcy expanded imperial discretion.

## After

Councils, courts, princely reforms, associations, and dissent created new politics inside an authoritarian and unequal imperial federation.

## Act I - Queen's Proclamation

Its promises addressed princes and subjects after mass violence but did not establish equal citizenship.

## Act II - Bureaucratic Raj

District officers, police, courts, and Indian clerks made distant rules local.

## Act III - Princely variety

Mysore, Baroda, Travancore, Bhopal, Kashmir, and tiny states differed in capacity and accountability.

## Act IV - Race and spectacle

Cantonments, clubs, durbars, and legal distinctions staged imperial rank.

## Act V - Limited representation

Councils and municipal institutions admitted selected Indians while preserving executive control.

## Subcontinental note

British India and princely India were interdependent; Baluchistan, the Northeast frontier, Kashmir, and coastal states had distinct arrangements.

## How we know

Proclamations, residency records, administrative reports, vernacular newspapers, princely archives, photographs, buildings, and petitions expose official and subject views.

## Do not claim

- Crown rule ended coercion after 1857.
- All princely states were the same.
- Indirect rule preserved untouched tradition.
- Indian officials merely copied British orders.
