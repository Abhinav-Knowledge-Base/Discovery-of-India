# Ep 100 — The Rashtrakutas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Rashtrakuta courts at Manyakheta project power from the Deccan to Kannauj while Kannada, Sanskrit, Jain, and temple cultures flourish through many patrons. |
| **Years** | c. 753–973 CE |
| **Before–after** | Badami Chalukya order → Rashtrakuta ascendancy and Western Chalukya succession |

## Before
Deccan authority rested on Chalukya institutions, local chiefs, Ganga and other allies, ports, agrarian zones, and forest corridors.

## The turn
Dantidurga and Krishna I replace Chalukya paramountcy; successors intervene north and south without sustaining uniform rule over every campaign zone.

## After
Tailapa II displaces the dynasty, while administrative, literary, artistic, and landed networks continue under new courts.

## Act I — Making a dynasty
Interrogate Rashtrakuta origin traditions and the many earlier families using the same political designation.

## Act II — Kailasa cut downward
Follow planning, quarrying, sculpting, painting, labor, and patronage at Ellora beyond the marvel of royal command.

## Act III — Riding toward Kannauj
Compare Dhruva and Govinda III’s praise with Pala and Pratihara records to show temporary supremacy.

## Act IV — Manyakheta’s languages
Set Amoghavarsha’s court, *Kavirajamarga*, Sanskrit learning, Jain teachers, and local speech in a multilingual world.

## Act V — Networks of rule
Examine feudatories, queens, merchants, grants, cavalry, cultivators, and forest communities without making “feudal” a complete explanation.

## Subcontinental note
Rashtrakuta links reached Gujarat, Bengal, Tamilakam, Sri Lankan diplomacy, and Arabian Sea trade, but campaign claims did not annex these histories.

## How we know
Inscriptions, grants, monuments, literary works, coins, and Arab geographers survive unevenly and often celebrate patrons.

## Do not claim
- Do not treat all Rashtrakuta-named lineages as one family.
- Do not say Kailasa was built by one king’s labor or in one instant.
- Do not map northern campaigns as permanent Deccan administration.
- Do not label Amoghavarsha exclusively Jain or Hindu.
- Do not reduce regional power to a single feudal hierarchy.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Dantidurga | Founder of imperial Rashtrakuta power |
| Krishna I | Patron associated with Ellora’s Kailasa |
| Amoghavarsha I | Long-reigning ruler and literary patron |
| Manyakheta | Rashtrakuta capital in Karnataka |
| Kailasa temple | Monolithic monument involving complex labor |
| *Kavirajamarga* | Early Kannada poetics and regional literary geography |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Ellora quarry marks, Manyakheta, Deccan roads, Kannada and Sanskrit inscriptions, coins, and port evidence; distinguish reign dates from building phases.
