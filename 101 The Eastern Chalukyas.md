# Ep 101 — The Eastern Chalukyas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | In the Vengi delta, Eastern Chalukya rulers survive centuries of pressure while Telugu inscription and literature gain courtly reach. |
| **Years** | c. 624–1070 CE |
| **Before–after** | Badami Chalukya eastern conquest → Vengi–Chola dynastic integration |

## Before
The Krishna–Godavari region held fertile deltas, Buddhist sites, ports, upland and forest communities, and Vishnukundin political inheritances.

## The turn
Kubja Vishnuvardhana establishes a durable branch whose succession becomes entangled with Rashtrakuta, Western Chalukya, and Chola ambitions.

## After
Rajendra Chalukya becomes Chola emperor Kulottunga I, joining Vengi and Chola succession without erasing Andhra institutions.

## Act I — Delta as infrastructure
Follow channels, monsoons, rice fields, ports, and labor rather than treating fertility as automatic geography.

## Act II — A branch becomes local
Use changing titles, marriage, grants, and centers to show adaptation beyond a permanent Badami offshoot identity.

## Act III — Succession under pressure
Map rival claimants and external backing without reducing Vengi to a chessboard for larger empires.

## Act IV — Telugu becomes monumental
Track Telugu’s growing inscriptional use and Nannaya’s *Mahabharata* without declaring one birth date for the language.

## Act V — Faith and settlement
Set Shaiva and Vaishnava temples beside Buddhist survivals, Jain presence, caste formation, women patrons, and forest margins.

## Subcontinental note
Vengi connected Odisha, Karnataka, Tamilakam, Sri Lankan-linked sea routes, and Southeast Asia as an active region, not a buffer.

## How we know
Copper plates, stone inscriptions, temples, literary manuscripts, coins, and Chola records produce detailed but disputed genealogies.

## Do not claim
- Do not call Eastern Chalukyas merely puppets of Badami, Rashtrakutas, or Cholas.
- Do not date the “birth of Telugu” to one inscription or poet.
- Do not assume delta land was naturally or fully cultivated.
- Do not equate dynastic marriage with cultural annexation.
- Do not erase Buddhist and Jain continuities from temple growth.
