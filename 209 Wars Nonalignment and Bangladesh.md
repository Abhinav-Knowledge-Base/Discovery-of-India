## Before

Partition left disputed borders, divided armed forces, refugee flows, and a Cold War order that pressured new states to choose blocs.

## The turn

Wars in 1947-48, 1962, 1965, and 1971 tested institutions while nonalignment pursued autonomy through changing partnerships.

## After

Bangladesh emerged after Pakistan's political crisis, mass violence, Bengali resistance, refugee flight, and Indian military intervention.

## Act I - Nonalignment in practice

Bandung and the Non-Aligned Movement pursued decolonization and room for maneuver, not neutrality on every issue.

## Act II - China and 1962

Competing border claims, Tibet, failed diplomacy, and military unpreparedness produced defeat and political shock.

## Act III - 1965 war

Kashmir-centered escalation involved India and Pakistan, uncertain gains, civilian costs, and Tashkent diplomacy.

## Act IV - Bangladesh crisis

The 1970 election's denial, Pakistani military violence, Mukti Bahini resistance, refugees, and global diplomacy preceded intervention.

## Act V - War and institutions

Parliament, press, armed forces, intelligence, and civilian leadership learned unevenly while border communities bore recurring costs.

## Subcontinental note

Kashmir, Ladakh, Arunachal frontier, Punjab, Rajasthan, East Pakistan/Bangladesh, and the Indian Ocean had distinct strategic and human histories.

## How we know

Official histories, diplomatic cables, parliamentary debate, maps, soldiers' letters, refugee testimony, foreign archives, and war graves contain national biases.

## Do not claim

- Nonalignment meant passive neutrality.
- 1962 had one simple cause.
- 1971 was only an India-Pakistan war.
- Military victory erased refugee trauma or Bangladesh's agency.
