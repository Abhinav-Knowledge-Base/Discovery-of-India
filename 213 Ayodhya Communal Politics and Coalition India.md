## Before

Post-Emergency politics included Congress dominance under strain, regional movements, caste mobilization, insurgencies, and unresolved communal violence.

## The turn

Campaigns to build a Ram temple, state decisions, televised imagery, and organized processions culminated in the Babri Masjid's demolition in 1992.

## After

Riots, litigation, electoral realignment, and coalition cabinets followed; the 2019 judgment and later temple did not erase historical disagreement or loss.

## Act I - Making a national campaign

Organizations, parties, pilgrims, media, and processions turned a disputed site into mass mobilization.

## Act II - Demolition and responsibility

The mosque fell despite assurances and state presence; inquiries and cases examined organized action and administrative failure.

## Act III - Communal aftermath

Mumbai 1992-93 and Gujarat 2002 require specific evidence, victims' perspectives, and distinction among events.

## Act IV - Coalition era

United Front, NDA, Congress alliances, and regional parties made federal bargaining central to governance.

## Act V - Court and memory

Archaeology, title suits, the 2019 Supreme Court decision, and competing sacred histories operate under different standards.

## Subcontinental note

Ayodhya connected to politics across India, but Kashmir, Punjab, the Northeast, Tamil Nadu, and regional-party states followed their own political timelines.

## How we know

Court records, Liberhan Commission material, election data, party publications, video, newspapers, archaeological reports, survivor testimony, and fact-finding reports conflict.

## Do not claim

- Ancient evidence alone mechanically decides modern title law.
- All Hindus or Muslims held one political view.
- Every riot had the same causes.
- Coalition government meant weak or illegitimate government.
