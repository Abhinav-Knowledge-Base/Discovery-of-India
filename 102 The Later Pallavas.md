# Ep 102 — The Later Pallavas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Later Pallava rulers rebuild authority through temples, brahmadeya networks, and alliances even as Pandyas, Rashtrakutas, and Cholas narrow their power. |
| **Years** | c. 700–897 CE |
| **Before–after** | Rajasimha’s monumental court → Pallava contraction and Chola takeover |

## Before
Kanchipuram and northern Tamilakam contained temple institutions, Jain and Buddhist communities, merchant routes, agrarian settlements, and local assemblies.

## The turn
Nandivarman II gains a contested throne and sustains a dynasty through war, marriage, and local intermediaries.

## After
Aparajita defeats Pandyas with Chola support but is later displaced by Aditya I, ending major Pallava sovereignty.

## Act I — A boy from a collateral line
Read Vaikuntha Perumal reliefs and inscriptions as dynastic staging, not a literal illustrated chronicle.

## Act II — Kanchi after Mamalla
Show a living city of temples, monasteries, crafts, water systems, and neighborhoods beyond royal monuments.

## Act III — Assemblies and grants
Explore brahmadeyas and local bodies as negotiated and unequal institutions; avoid making them timeless democratic villages.

## Act IV — War without finality
Follow shifting Pallava, Pandya, Ganga, Rashtrakuta, and Chola coalitions rather than two fixed ethnic camps.

## Act V — After the dynasty
Trace Pallava titles, artisans, institutions, and sacred sites into Chola rule without claiming cultural replacement.

## Subcontinental note
Later Pallava maritime and religious connections with Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia joined independent regions and cannot be summarized as colonization.

## How we know
Temple inscriptions, sculptural programs, copper plates, devotional literature, archaeology, and rival records leave succession and battle details contested.

## Do not claim
- Do not read Vaikuntha Perumal panels as straightforward reportage.
- Do not call local assemblies universally democratic.
- Do not treat brahmadeya expansion as one simple feudal process.
- Do not frame Pallava–Pandya warfare as modern Tamil identity politics.
- Do not say Pallava culture vanished when sovereignty ended.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Kanchipuram’s Vaikuntha Perumal and Kailasanatha temples, tanks, inscriptions, village boundaries, and battle-route landscapes; annotate restoration.
