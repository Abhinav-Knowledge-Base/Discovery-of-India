## Before

Economic strain, protest movements, centralization, and a court judgment intensified conflict around Indira Gandhi's government.

## The turn

The 1975 proclamation enabled censorship, detention, constitutional amendments, slum clearances, and coercive sterilization with major regional variation.

## After

The 1977 election removed the government and restored competition, but institutional scars, fragmented opposition, and unresolved executive power remained.

## Act I - Proclamation and law

Article 352, presidential action, Parliament, courts, and bureaucracy converted a political crisis into authoritarian rule.

## Act II - Censorship and detention

Journalists, activists, opposition leaders, workers, and ordinary detainees experienced unequal surveillance and imprisonment.

## Act III - Bodies and cities

Sterilization quotas and Delhi demolitions targeted poor and marginalized communities, though implementation varied by state.

## Act IV - Resistance and accommodation

Underground networks, civil liberties groups, regional leaders, and some officials resisted while others complied or benefited.

## Act V - Election and aftermath

The Janata victory demonstrated electoral recovery; internal division and later constitutional safeguards showed both strength and fragility.

## Subcontinental note

Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Bihar, Kerala, the Northeast, Kashmir, and Delhi differed in repression, opposition, and electoral response; no capital-only story is adequate.

## How we know

Gazette notifications, court judgments, Shah Commission testimony, newspapers with blank spaces, prison memoirs, health records, photographs, and oral histories document coercion.

## Do not claim

- The Emergency abolished all institutions in identical ways.
- Every official and citizen either resisted or supported it.
- Sterilization was voluntary family welfare.
- The 1977 election permanently removed authoritarian risk.
