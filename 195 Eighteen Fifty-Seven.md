## Before

Annexations, military rules, revenue pressures, dispossessed courts, and religious anxieties accumulated differently across Company territories.

## The turn

The Meerut mutiny moved to Delhi, but Awadh, Bundelkhand, Rohilkhand, Bihar, and central India developed distinct coalitions and aims.

## After

Brutal suppression ended Company rule, expanded racial authoritarianism, and generated rival memories of rebellion, loyalty, and loss.

## Act I - Meerut and Delhi

Sepoys proclaimed Bahadur Shah Zafar while civilians, princes, and city groups faced siege and scarcity.

## Act II - Awadh

Taluqdars, peasants, sepoys, Begum Hazrat Mahal, and local leaders made a broad but divided war.

## Act III - Bundelkhand and central India

Rani Lakshmibai, Tantia Tope, rulers, and soldiers pursued shifting alliances.

## Act IV - Other Indias

Punjab recruitment, Bengal's limited rising, Bombay and Madras caution, and southern rumors show uneven participation.

## Act V - Reprisals and remembrance

Executions, village destruction, exile, and British retribution shaped archives and later national memory.

## Subcontinental note

The rebellion's geography included present India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nepal connections; absence of revolt also needs explanation.

## How we know

Rebel proclamations, Urdu newspapers, letters, court-martial files, photographs, British memoirs, oral traditions, and archaeology disagree sharply.

## Do not claim

- All India rose as one nation.
- The cartridge issue alone caused revolt.
- All rebels shared aims and social interests.
- British accounts of atrocities are neutral while Indian suffering is unknowable.
