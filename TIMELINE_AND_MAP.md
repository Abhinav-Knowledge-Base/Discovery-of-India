## Map grammar

1. **Base before polity:** relief, rivers, coast, monsoon, soils and routes precede political overlays.
2. **No anachronistic borders:** present national/state outlines are absent before their historical creation.
3. **Evidence points:** dated inscriptions, coins, archaeology and textual attestations appear as distinct symbols.
4. **Uncertainty:** solid = directly evidenced; gradient = interpreted reach; hatching = disputed; blank = unknown, not empty.
5. **Parallel lanes:** northwest, Ganga-Himalaya, west/central, Deccan, south/ocean, east/northeast and islands remain visible.
6. **Power vocabulary:** “capital,” “court,” “garrison,” “tributary claim,” “route” and “findspot” are not interchangeable.
7. **Time:** every frame shows a date or range. Slow change uses small multiples, not seamless morphing.
8. **Scale:** global locator → subcontinental relation → regional system → site. The reverse move closes sequences.
9. **Names:** first historically attested form where appropriate; modern locator in parentheses. Competing names may coexist.
10. **Contemporary disputes:** follow legal review, mark claims without endorsing them and explain the convention.

## Changing geographic words

| Term | Historical caution | Production rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhārata/Bharat** | Appears in textual, genealogical and geographic registers of changing scale; not automatically the modern republic | Translate/contextualize each occurrence; do not back-project constitutional “India, that is Bharat” |
| **India/Indikē/al-Hind** | Exonyms and transregional terms derived through the Indus/Sindhu complex; scope varies by speaker | Identify author, language, date and intended zone |
| **Hind/Hindustan** | Can indicate lands east of the Indus, a broad region, north Indian realm, Mughal geography or later homeland; meanings overlap | Never assign one fixed religious or national meaning |
| **Jambudvipa** | Cosmological-geographic term with Buddhist, Jain and Brahmanical usages | Show cosmological frame; do not equate mechanically with a modern map |
| **Aryavarta/Dakshinapatha** | Normative/geographic terms whose boundaries vary | Attribute to source and period; do not treat as surveyed territories |
| **South Asia** | Modern analytical region with institutional and geopolitical histories | Use in present-day scholarly explanation, not as ancient self-description |
| **Subcontinent** | Useful for connected scale but can centre India and flatten neighbours | Say “historical subcontinent”; name Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka and Maldives directly |
| **India after 1947** | Sovereign republic with changing internal states and disputed borders | India-centred narrative; retain causal links to Pakistan/Bangladesh and wider neighbours |
