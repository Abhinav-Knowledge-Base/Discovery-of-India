## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Hampi becomes an imperial metropolis where sacred topography, commerce, water, and military spectacle sustain unequal power. |
| **Years** | c. 1377–1565, with later memory |
| **Before–after** | From a Sangama stronghold to a conquered capital whose living sacred landscape survives. |
## Before
- The early kingdom rests on negotiated authority across several linguistic regions.
- Hampi's landscape is sacred and inhabited before its imperial monumentalization.
## The turn
- Devaraya and Tuluva rulers enlarge fortifications, markets, canals, and courtly zones.
- Krishnadevaraya's campaigns and gifts make the capital a stage of universal claims.
## After
- The 1565 defeat prompts court dispersal, plunder, and political relocation.
- Worship and settlement continue, resisting the phrase “abandoned ruins.”
## Act I — Making a metropolis
- Reconstruct roads, walls, suburbs, tanks, fields, and granite quarries.
- Distinguish the sacred center from the royal center without treating either as sealed.
- Laborers and artisans, largely unnamed, materialize imperial ambition.
## Act II — Markets of the world
- Horses arrive through western ports; textiles, gems, grain, and metals circulate.
- Foreign visitors describe abundance through expectations of exotic kingship.
- Market streets also reveal taxation, brokerage, caste, and gendered work.
## Act III — Court and temple
- Virupaksha, Vitthala, and Hazara Rama complexes articulate different patronage.
- Ramayana reliefs turn epic narrative into a political language, not a transparent diary.
- Indo-Islamicate arches and courtly forms complicate civilizational binaries.
## Act IV — Water and force
- Channels and reservoirs support crops, gardens, ritual, and elite display.
- Elephant stables, platforms, gateways, and barracks stage coercive capacity.
- The city's prosperity depends on extraction from wider agrarian regions.
## Act V — 1565 and its afterlives
- Talikota involves shifting Deccan alliances rather than one religious bloc against another.
- Archaeology documents burning and damage but not a uniform single destruction event.
- End among active shrines and descendants for whom Hampi is not merely a ruin.
## Subcontinental note
- Capital forms converse with Deccan sultanates and Indian Ocean trade.
- Shared techniques coexist with war, enslavement, desecration, and unequal patronage.
## How we know
- Excavation and landscape survey recover production, water, and neighborhood patterns.
- Inscriptions reveal gifts and offices but privilege donors and institutions.
- Paes, Nuniz, and Razzaq require comparison with local material evidence.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Hampi was a uniformly planned city.
- Do not claim 1565 was a simple Hindu–Muslim war.
- Do not claim visitor estimates are precise population statistics.
- Do not claim “synthesis” erased violence or hierarchy.
