# Ep 94 — Sindh and the Arab Frontier

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | The Arab conquest of Sindh creates a negotiated frontier of garrisons, taxes, temples, trade, resistance, and translation—not a sudden civilizational replacement. |
| **Years** | c. 630–870 CE |
| **Before–after** | Rai and Chach polities → Umayyad conquest and Abbasid-era regional emirates |

## Before
The lower Indus connected Buddhist and Brahmanical institutions, Jat and Med communities, pastoralists, farmers, ports, and Iranian–Indian Ocean trade.

## The turn
Muhammad ibn Qasim’s forces capture key centers from Dahir, then govern through garrisons, tribute, local intermediaries, and uneven agreements.

## After
Mansura and Multan anchor regional Muslim-ruled polities whose autonomy, commerce, and cultural exchange grow beyond direct caliphal control.

## Act I — The river before conquest
Center Indus ecology and Sindh’s own political history rather than beginning with an army arriving from the west.

## Act II — Reading the *Chachnama*
Treat its Persian text as a thirteenth-century work drawing on earlier materials, not a verbatim eighth-century dispatch.

## Act III — Siege and settlement
Reconstruct Debal, Brahmanabad, and Multan while marking disputed identifications, casualty numbers, and literary tropes.

## Act IV — Governing difference
Examine taxes, protected status, conversion, temples, mosques, caste, gender, and local power as changing practices, not one fixed policy.

## Act V — A connected frontier
Follow Arabic–Sanskrit knowledge exchange, coinage, horses, textiles, and pilgrims through Sindh’s cities and countryside.

## Subcontinental note
Sindh is treated as an Indus and oceanic region in its own right, now largely in Pakistan, connected to but not rhetorically absorbed by later Indian borders.

## How we know
Arabic geographies, coins, inscriptions, archaeology, the late *Chachnama*, and sparse local records conflict over dates, places, and motives.

## Do not claim
- Do not narrate 711 as the first contact between Islam and South Asia.
- Do not use the *Chachnama* as an unproblematic eyewitness source.
- Do not claim conquest caused immediate mass conversion.
- Do not reduce all local communities to Hindu-versus-Muslim camps.
- Do not annex Sindh to a modern nation’s exclusive civilizational story.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Dahir | Last major ruler defeated in conquest narratives |
| Muhammad ibn Qasim | Umayyad commander surrounded by later legend |
| *Chachnama* | Essential but late Persian conquest narrative |
| Debal | Port whose exact archaeological identification is disputed |
| Brahmanabad / Mansura | Successive or overlapping centers with debated topography |
| Multan | Temple, trade, and frontier city under changing rule |

## Locations and visual evidence
Use Indus channels, Banbhore, Mansura mounds, coin legends, ceramics, and mosque remains; caption all disputed site identifications.
