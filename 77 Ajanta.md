# Ep 77 — Ajanta

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | In a ravine above the Waghora, caves built across centuries reveal monastic practice, courtly aspiration, labor, and global Buddhist storytelling. |
| **Years** | c. 2nd century BCE–6th century CE |
| **Before–after** | Early rock-cut monastic caves → Vakataka-era building surge and later reuse |

## Before
Early chaitya halls and viharas served Buddhist communities on Deccan routes long before the Vakataka court.

## The turn
In the fifth century, elite patrons, monks, planners, quarry workers, painters, and sculptors transform the ravine at remarkable speed.

## After
Construction slows and patronage changes; worship and memory persist before the caves’ modern archaeological and tourist afterlives.

## Act I — Entering the ravine
Let geology, water, paths, darkness, and acoustics establish Ajanta as an inhabited environment rather than a gallery of masterpieces.

## Act II — Two building horizons
Distinguish early caves from fifth-century interventions and show how unfinished surfaces preserve sequences of work.

## Act III — Painting a world
Read Jataka narratives, court scenes, foreigners, textiles, animals, and gestures as crafted images, not transparent ethnographic photographs.

## Act IV — Who paid, who worked?
Set Varahadeva and Upendragupta beside anonymous artisans, monastics, suppliers, women donors, and nearby agrarian and forest communities.

## Act V — Conservation and rediscovery
Address colonial copying, damage, changing pigments, tourism, and the ethics of filming fragile interiors.

## Subcontinental note
Ajanta participated in Buddhist routes joining the Deccan to Sri Lanka, Central Asia, and maritime worlds without proving a single uniform Buddhist culture.

## How we know
Donative inscriptions, stratigraphy, architecture, pigments, tool marks, narrative comparison, and conservation records support chronologies that scholars still debate.

## Do not claim
- Do not date every cave or painting to one king’s reign with false precision.
- Do not call Ajanta a “lost city” discovered by Europeans.
- Do not identify painted figures’ caste or ethnicity solely by appearance.
- Do not make elite court scenes representative of all gendered lives.
- Do not erase artisans and local ecologies behind patron-centered language.

## Locations and visual evidence
Use exterior drone footage only where permitted, low-light interiors, unfinished pillars, pigments, watercourses, and conservation labs; never add damaging illumination.
