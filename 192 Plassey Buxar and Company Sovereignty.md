## Before

The Company was a privileged merchant corporation dependent on Indian producers, bankers, sepoys, and political permission.

## The turn

Plassey installed an ally; Mir Qasim's resistance and the Buxar coalition widened the contest beyond Bengal.

## After

The 1765 diwani fused trade with revenue collection, obscured responsibility, and financed further war.

## Act I - Plassey's conspiracy

Clive's compact with Mir Jafar, bankers, merchants, and commanders outweighed battlefield exchange.

## Act II - Puppet costs

Company demands destabilized the treasury and exposed limits of indirect control.

## Act III - Mir Qasim's challenge

Reform and equal customs rules threatened private privilege.

## Act IV - Buxar's coalition

The Company defeated Mir Qasim, Awadh, and Shah Alam using Indian troops and finance.

## Act V - Diwani and dual rule

A Mughal grant under coercive conditions gave revenue rights without clear responsibility.

## Subcontinental note

Bengal and Bihar connected to Awadh, Delhi, and Britain; revenue from cultivators funded armies of largely Indian sepoys.

## How we know

Persian accounts, treaties, minutes, private letters, revenue data, coins, and battlefield topography reveal conflicting interests.

## Do not claim

- Plassey alone created the Raj.
- Victory proved inherent European superiority.
- Indian allies lacked agendas.
- Diwani instantly made a uniform colonial state.
