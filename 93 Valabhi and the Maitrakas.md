# Ep 93 — Valabhi and the Maitrakas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Valabhi anchors a western Indian kingdom where ports, grants, Jain and Buddhist learning, and Brahmanical patronage overlap. |
| **Years** | c. 475–776 CE |
| **Before–after** | Gupta and post-Kshatrapa Gujarat → Maitraka rule and its uncertain end |

## Before
Saurashtra and mainland Gujarat linked pastoral zones, farms, craft centers, ports, monasteries, and merchant routes to the Arabian Sea.

## The turn
Bhatarka and successors convert military office into dynastic rule, governing through regional elites and a prolific charter culture.

## After
Valabhi’s dynasty disappears amid poorly documented conflict, while ports, religious institutions, and successor states continue.

## Act I — A capital from fragments
Use Valabhi’s mounds, seals, and texts to resist reconstructing a famous university as a fully known campus.

## Act II — Reading the charters
Hundreds of grants reveal formulas, officials, villages, and donees while underrepresenting cultivators and prior users.

## Act III — Monastery and curriculum
Xuanzang and Yijing report Buddhist learning; Jain traditions also make Valabhi a textual landmark.

## Act IV — Sea and hinterland
Connect ports, cotton, salt, livestock, crafts, and agrarian expansion to forest and pastoral communities.

## Act V — Ending without a date
Compare Arab campaigns and later traditions, leaving Valabhi’s destruction and final chronology open.

## Subcontinental note
Valabhi faced Sindh, Rajasthan, Malwa, Deccan powers, and Arabian Sea routes as a western center, not a remnant outside “classical India.”

## How we know
Copper plates, seals, archaeology, Jain traditions, Chinese pilgrims, and later chronicles provide abundance in some reigns and silence at the end.

## Do not claim
- Do not call Valabhi a university identical to a modern institution.
- Do not assert one exact destruction event without qualification.
- Do not equate charter survival with uniform territorial control.
- Do not infer religious exclusivity from a ruler’s chosen titles.
- Do not describe pastoral or forest lands as vacant grant space.
