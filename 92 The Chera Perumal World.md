# Ep 92 — The Chera Perumal World

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Kerala’s Perumal era emerges through temples, assemblies, ports, and layered Jewish, Christian, Muslim, Jain, Buddhist, and Brahmanical connections. |
| **Years** | c. 800–1124 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-early-Chera regional formations → Chera Perumal order and successor principalities |

## Before
Western coastal and upland communities exchanged pepper, forest products, rice, craft goods, and ritual services through changing local authorities.

## The turn
Rulers at Mahodayapuram interact with temple corporations, Brahmin settlements, merchant bodies, and port communities.

## After
Perumal authority fragments or transforms into regional polities as Chola pressure and maritime commerce reshape the coast.

## Act I — Reconstructing Mahodayapuram
Build the capital from archaeology, inscriptions, waterways, and contested identifications rather than a seamless palace narrative.

## Act II — Temple and assembly
Examine *ur*, *sabha*, and temple institutions as sites of coordination and exclusion, not proof of timeless village democracy.

## Act III — Copper-plate coast
Read the Tharisapalli and Jewish plates as negotiated privileges involving witnesses, services, taxes, and multilingual port society.

## Act IV — Hills behind pepper
Bring cultivators, forest communities, carriers, and ecological knowledge into a trade story too often confined to merchants and ships.

## Act V — A plural shore
Show religious coexistence alongside hierarchy and competition; avoid modern communal boundaries.

## Subcontinental note
Kerala’s ties to Tamilakam, Sri Lanka, Arabia, East Africa, and the Persian Gulf formed reciprocal oceanic histories, not isolation or unilateral foreign impact.

## How we know
Vatteluttu inscriptions, copper plates, temple records, archaeology, foreign notices, and later legends leave chronology and royal succession disputed.

## Do not claim
- Do not treat the Cheraman Perumal legends as contemporary biography.
- Do not date every Jewish or Christian tradition solely from later memory.
- Do not call assemblies democratic in the modern inclusive sense.
- Do not reduce Kerala’s social orders to one land-grant cause.
- Do not erase forest producers from the pepper economy.
