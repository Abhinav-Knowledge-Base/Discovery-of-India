## Before

At independence, British paramountcy lapsed over more than five hundred states whose rulers and subjects faced unequal political choices.

## The turn

Instruments of Accession joined most states to India, while Junagadh, Hyderabad, Kashmir, and people's movements produced distinct crises.

## After

Merger and constitutional reorganization reduced princely sovereignty, but Kashmir's conflict and regional demands remained unresolved.

## Act I - Instruments and bargaining

Patel, Menon, rulers, and state peoples negotiated accession over defense, communications, and external affairs.

## Act II - Popular sovereignty

Praja Mandals and local movements challenged rulers; integration was not only diplomacy from above.

## Act III - Junagadh and Hyderabad

Plebiscite, blockade, Razakar violence, Telangana struggle, and armed action followed different sequences.

## Act IV - Jammu and Kashmir

Accession followed invasion and war; Article 370, UN involvement, regional differences, and displacement structured dispute.

## Act V - Making the map

Covenants, privy purses, centrally administered areas, and later state boundaries converted layered sovereignty into a Union.

## Subcontinental note

Travancore, Manipur, Bhopal, Sikkim, the Northeast frontier, and Kashmir show that no single accession model covered the subcontinent.

## How we know

Instruments, cabinet papers, princely archives, UN records, military reports, Praja Mandal sources, oral histories, and refugee testimony conflict.

## Do not claim

- Every state integrated peacefully.
- Rulers alone determined popular wishes.
- Kashmir's accession settled every political question.
- Hyderabad and Junagadh followed identical paths.
