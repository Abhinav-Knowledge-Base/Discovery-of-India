## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Humayun loses Hindustan, survives through family and Safavid power, and returns with a transformed Timurid court. |
| **Years** | 1530–1556 |
| **Before–after** | From insecure inheritance through exile to brief restoration. |
## Before
- Babur's conquests leave powerful Afghan rivals and untested succession.
- Timurid custom gives brothers appanages and opportunities to rebel.
## The turn
- Sher Khan defeats Humayun at Chausa and Kannauj.
- Flight through Rajasthan and Sind turns imperial family politics into survival.
## After
- Safavid aid enables recovery of Kandahar and Kabul.
- Humayun retakes Delhi in 1555 but dies months later.
## Act I — Divided inheritance
- Kamran, Askari, and Hindal hold commands within contested dynastic expectations.
- Humayun's Gujarat campaign disperses effort without securing the east.
- Astrology shaped court timing but does not explain defeat alone.
## Act II — Afghan ascent
- Sher Khan builds revenue, fort, and military networks in Bihar and Bengal.
- Chausa exposes Mughal logistical weakness.
- Kannauj ends Humayun's first reign, not Timurid ambition.
## Act III — Exile and Hamida Banu
- Hamida Banu Begum gives birth to Akbar at Umarkot amid flight.
- Elite women manage kinship, travel, resources, and mediation.
- Exile also burdens servants, soldiers, and host communities.
## Act IV — Safavid bargain
- Shah Tahmasp supplies status and forces under negotiated religious ceremony.
- Kandahar becomes a contested hinge between empires.
- Persianate influence predates exile but intensifies through people and practice.
## Act V — Return and fall
- Humayun and Bairam Khan exploit Sur fragmentation.
- Sirhind opens Delhi and Agra, not the whole subcontinent.
- The library-stair death contrasts restoration with dynastic fragility.
## Subcontinental note
- Exile embeds the Mughals in Iranian and Central Asian diplomacy.
- Restoration depends on Afghan division and Indian resources, not destiny.
## How we know
- Gulbadan Begum preserves family memory with selective intimacy.
- Persian court histories frame Safavid support differently.
- Coins, architecture, letters, and campaign routes test narrative chronology.
## Do not claim
- Do not portray Humayun as merely incompetent.
- Do not erase Hamida Banu and Gulbadan's political labor.
- Do not call Safavid aid unconditional.
- Do not equate the 1555 return with stable control.
