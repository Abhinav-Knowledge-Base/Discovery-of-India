## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Shah Jahan orders court, city, and stone into a vision of sovereignty financed by agrarian extraction and sustained by war. |
| **Years** | 1628–1658 |
| **Before–after** | From violent succession to another succession war and imperial confinement. |
## Before
- Prince Khurram wins through military success, rebellion, and Asaf Khan's support.
- Rivals are eliminated in accordance with harsh Timurid succession practice.
## The turn
- A more formal court hierarchy and monumental program center the emperor.
- Shahjahanabad relocates the capital to a new Delhi urban complex.
## After
- Kandahar campaigns fail and Deccan commitments continue.
- Illness in 1657 triggers war among Dara, Shuja, Murad, and Aurangzeb.
## Act I — Ordering the court
- Peacock Throne ceremony makes rank and access spatially visible.
- Jharokha, gifts, robes, and mansabs bind nobles to the sovereign.
- Formal order masks competition for assignments.
## Act II — Building empire
- Taj Mahal is a dynastic mausoleum, garden, workshop, and managed estate.
- Red Fort and Jama Masjid organize palace, city, commerce, and congregational life.
- Craftspeople and laborers must appear alongside patrons.
## Act III — Money and countryside
- Monumental spending draws on a large agrarian revenue system.
- Market growth and silver circulation coexist with local scarcity and extraction.
- The 1630–32 famine exposes ecological and administrative vulnerability.
## Act IV — Frontiers
- Balkh and Badakhshan campaigns consume men and treasure.
- Safavids recover Kandahar in 1649 despite repeated Mughal attempts.
- Deccan settlements manage rather than resolve regional power.
## Act V — Succession war
- Dara Shukoh's position at court does not guarantee military support.
- Aurangzeb and Murad defeat rivals through coalition and strategy.
- Shah Jahan's confinement turns filial conflict into a sovereignty dispute.
## Subcontinental note
- Architectural coherence should not be mistaken for uniform political control.
- Mughal aesthetics draw on Persianate, Indic, Central Asian, and European-linked workshops without frictionless “synthesis.”
## How we know
- *Padshahnama* manuscripts stage ideal sovereignty.
- Accounts, inscriptions, travelers, workshop evidence, and famine reports reveal other costs.
- Taj labor figures and mutilation legends require evidence, not repetition.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Shah Jahan bankrupted the empire by buildings alone.
- Do not repeat the Taj Mahal artisan-amputation myth.
- Do not call Dara a modern secular liberal.
- Do not equate court order with social harmony.
