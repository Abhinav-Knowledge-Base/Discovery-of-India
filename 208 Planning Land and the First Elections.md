## Before

Partition disruption, food shortage, low life expectancy, concentrated landholding, and weak infrastructure framed independence.

## The turn

The first elections enrolled an overwhelmingly new electorate while Five-Year Plans and state projects sought rapid development.

## After

Democracy gained durable procedures, yet uneven land reform, displacement, bureaucratic power, and regional disparities limited transformation.

## Act I - The electoral wager

Officials, parties, women, Dalits, Adivasis, and remote communities made the 1951-52 vote logistically and politically consequential.

## Act II - Land reform

Abolition of zamindari, tenancy laws, ceilings, and Bhoodan varied by state and were reshaped by courts and landed power.

## Act III - Planning choices

Heavy industry, dams, community development, and public institutions reflected scarcity, sovereignty, and contested economic ideas.

## Act IV - Science and health

Institutes, atomic energy, disease control, and family planning expanded state ambition with ethical and access disputes.

## Act V - Costs and critics

Dam displacement, forest loss, tribal dispossession, labor conflict, and neglected primary needs challenged development's distribution.

## Subcontinental note

Kerala, West Bengal, Kashmir, the Northeast, central Adivasi regions, and former princely states encountered planning and democracy differently.

## How we know

Election reports, Planning Commission papers, village studies, land records, parliamentary debate, health data, film, and displaced people's testimony test official claims.

## Do not claim

- The first election was an effortless success.
- Planning followed one uncontested socialist blueprint.
- Land reform abolished rural hierarchy everywhere.
- Large dams delivered benefits without displacement.
