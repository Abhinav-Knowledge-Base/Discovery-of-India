## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Brahmaputra and Imphal valley states grow through migration, wet-rice systems, warfare, and negotiated incorporation. |
| **Years** | c. 1228–1600 |
| **Before–after** | From diverse valley and upland polities to expanding Ahom and Meitei courts. |
## Before
- Assam and Manipur contain many Tibeto-Burman, Tai, Indo-Aryan, and other communities.
- Kamarupa's legacy does not imply one continuous regional state.
## The turn
- Sukapha crosses the Patkai and establishes an Ahom foothold in 1228.
- Meitei kings consolidate institutions in the Imphal valley through clan negotiation.
## After
- Ahom rule expands through incorporation, conflict, and administrative adaptation.
- Manipur's court strengthens while hill-valley relations remain reciprocal and contested.
## Act I — Rivers and passes
- Let monsoon, floodplain, hills, and routes define political possibilities.
- Migration is organized settlement, not empty-land colonization.
- Existing Moran, Borahi, Naga, and other communities retain agency.
## Act II — Ahom institutions
- The paik system organizes labor and military obligation through households.
- Wet-rice expertise and embankments support expansion.
- Office and clan systems change as populations enter Ahom authority.
## Act III — Buranji memory
- Ahom chronicles record diplomacy, succession, omens, and war.
- Surviving manuscripts are copied and revised, not transparent originals.
- Compare them with archaeology, language, and neighboring records.
## Act IV — Meitei court
- Ningthouja rulers compete with other valley clans and neighboring polities.
- The *Cheitharol Kumbaba* offers a court chronology with layered redaction.
- Indigenous ritual institutions precede later Vaishnava court transformation.
## Act V — Connected frontiers
- Trade, marriage, raids, tribute, and captive movement cross hill-valley boundaries.
- Firearms and new diplomacy gradually alter regional competition.
- End before Mughal-centered narratives turn the northeast into a margin.
## Subcontinental note
- These are sovereign historical worlds, not merely “tribal frontiers.”
- Later ethnic and national borders should not be projected backward.
## How we know
- Buranjis and Meitei chronicles preserve court viewpoints and later editing.
- Archaeology, ceramics, ecological history, oral traditions, and linguistics fill major gaps.
- Colonial ethnography is late evidence and carries classificatory bias.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Sukapha entered an uninhabited land.
- Do not claim Ahom incorporation was always peaceful assimilation.
- Do not claim hill peoples lacked states or diplomacy.
- Do not claim current ethnic identities map unchanged onto medieval names.
