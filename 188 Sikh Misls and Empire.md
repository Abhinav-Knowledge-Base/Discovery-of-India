## Before

The Khalsa and Banda Singh Bahadur challenged Mughal authority while Sikh communities remained socially diverse.

## The turn

Jathas and misls survived invasion and built territory before Ranjit Singh consolidated Lahore-centered rule.

## After

Succession conflict and Anglo-Sikh wars ended the empire, but institutions and memories crossed colonial borders.

## Act I - After the Gurus

Gurdwaras, mobile bands, peasant support, and discipline sustained community.

## Act II - Misl sovereignty

Warrior groups held territories through shifting alliances, not a central chain.

## Act III - Ranjit Singh's settlement

The court balanced chiefs, Dogras, diverse officials, and European officers.

## Act IV - Empire and frontier

Revenue and campaigns extended across Punjab, Kashmir, Multan, and Peshawar.

## Act V - Wars and annexation

Court instability and Company resources together shaped the 1845-49 conflicts.

## Subcontinental note

The story belongs to Indian and Pakistani Punjab, Kashmir, Ladakh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and diasporic Sikh memory.

## How we know

Gurmukhi and Persian chronicles, hukamnamas, files, paintings, arms, buildings, and British reports must be compared.

## Do not claim

- Misls formed one centralized state.
- The Lahore empire was religiously homogeneous.
- Annexation followed only internal weakness.
- Modern borders can be projected backward.
