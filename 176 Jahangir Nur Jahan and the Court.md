## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Jahangir's court turns justice, natural history, painting, and dynastic marriage into power while Nur Jahan governs within factional crisis. |
| **Years** | 1605–1627 |
| **Before–after** | From contested succession to Shah Jahan's victory after rebellion. |
## Before
- Prince Salim rebels before Akbar's death and cultivates his own household.
- Mughal succession lacks primogeniture and invites armed competition.
## The turn
- Jahangir secures the throne, defeats Khusrau, and marries Nur Jahan in 1611.
- Her family and allies gain offices, coin legends, and diplomatic visibility.
## After
- Prince Khurram rebels as court coalitions shift.
- Jahangir's death triggers a managed succession won by Shah Jahan.
## Act I — Justice and punishment
- The chain of justice stages direct access to the emperor.
- Khusrau's defeat brings exemplary punishment and Guru Arjan's execution.
- Memoir candor does not make imperial justice impartial.
## Act II — Nur Jahan's authority
- She issues farmans, appears on coins, hunts, brokers marriage, and sponsors buildings.
- Her power rests on imperial marriage, household resources, and allies.
- The label “junta” can oversimplify shifting coalitions.
## Act III — Seeing nature
- Jahangir records animals, plants, weather, and wonders with close observation.
- Painters such as Mansur combine empirical detail with courtly display.
- Collecting nature also expresses imperial possession.
## Act IV — Diplomacy and frontiers
- Mewar reaches settlement while Deccan and Kandahar remain difficult.
- English ambassador Thomas Roe seeks trade privileges with limited leverage.
- Gift exchange is political negotiation, not picturesque ceremony.
## Act V — Rebellion and succession
- Shah Jahan's campaigns and rebellion expose fiscal and dynastic strain.
- Mahabat Khan briefly seizes Jahangir; Nur Jahan maneuvers against him.
- Asaf Khan secures Shah Jahan after Jahangir's death.
## Subcontinental note
- Court influence extends through households, provinces, and envoys but remains negotiated.
- Women's political agency should not be explained as secret exception.
## How we know
- The *Tuzuk-i Jahangiri* mixes diary, edited continuation, and royal image.
- Farmans, coins, paintings, European accounts, and buildings document Nur Jahan's authority.
- Court sources obscure nonelite costs of campaigns and luxury.
## Do not claim
- Do not reduce Jahangir to addiction and leisure.
- Do not call Nur Jahan the sole hidden ruler.
- Do not explain Guru Arjan's execution by one motive.
- Do not treat naturalistic painting as politically neutral science.
