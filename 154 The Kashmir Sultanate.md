## Before
- Kashmir possesses Sanskrit scholarship, temple institutions, landed elites, and unstable courts.
- Mongol-related invasion and internal conflict disrupt valley power.
## The turn
- Shah Mir takes the throne in 1339 after service within Kashmir's court.
- A Muslim dynasty grows from local succession, not an external conquest alone.
## After
- Zain-ul-Abidin's reign becomes a touchstone for patronage and remembered justice.
- Chak rule and factional war precede Akbar's annexation.
## Act I — Changing sovereignty
- Trace Rinchana, Kota Rani, and Shah Mir without forcing linear conversion stories.
- Elite marriage and office connect old and new regimes.
- Chronicles encode moral judgments about gender and legitimacy.
## Act II — Sikandar's contested reign
- Persian chronicles and later memory associate Sikandar with temple destruction.
- Establish evidence case by case and retain coercion where documented.
- Avoid both denial and totalizing civilizational narrative.
## Act III — Sufis and Rishis
- Sayyid Muhammad Madani and other migrants link Kashmir to Persianate networks.
- Nund Rishi traditions develop through vernacular institutions and later hagiography.
- “Shared spirituality” did not remove status, patronage, or doctrinal disagreement.
## Act IV — Zain-ul-Abidin
- Court patronage supports Sanskrit, Persian, crafts, irrigation, and translation.
- Return and restoration narratives contribute to Bud Shah's later reputation.
- Fiscal and military evidence must accompany stories of tolerance.
## Act V — Chaks and Mughals
- Chak elites rise amid faction and sectarian tensions.
- Mughal campaigns exploit internal disputes but face mountain logistics.
- Annexation alters sovereignty while local institutions and resistance persist.
## Subcontinental note
- Kashmir connects Central Asia, Tibet, Punjab, and the subcontinent through difficult routes.
- Sectarian identity is historically specific and not equivalent to modern communal nationalism.
## How we know
- Jonaraja and later Sanskrit chroniclers continue Kalhana with court biases.
- Persian chronicles and hagiographies reflect competing institutional memories.
- Temples, mosques, shrines, manuscripts, crafts, and landscape evidence check texts.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Islam came to Kashmir through one mechanism.
- Do not claim Sikandar's iconoclasm is wholly invented or universally documented.
- Do not claim Rishi traditions created timeless harmony.
- Do not claim Mughal annexation produced immediate consent.
