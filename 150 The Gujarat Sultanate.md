## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Gujarat's sultans bind fertile hinterlands, Jain financiers, port cities, and monumental Ahmedabad into an ocean-facing state. |
| **Years** | 1407–1573 |
| **Before–after** | From Delhi governorship to Mughal province after regional sovereignty. |
## Before
- Gujarat contains Solanki-Vaghela legacies, commercial towns, temples, and ports.
- Delhi's governors rely on local revenue and military intermediaries.
## The turn
- Zafar Khan Muzaffar asserts independence amid Delhi's weakness.
- Ahmad Shah founds Ahmedabad in 1411 and relocates political gravity.
## After
- Mahmud Begada enlarges the realm and monumentalizes Champaner.
- Factional conflict enables Akbar's conquest, followed by repeated resistance.
## Act I — Making Ahmedabad
- Sabarmati siting joins fort, mosque, market, neighborhoods, and older settlements.
- Ahmad Shah's mosque patronage employs local stoneworkers and spolia.
- Shared craft vocabularies do not by themselves prove social harmony.
## Act II — Merchants and ports
- Cambay, Diu, and other ports connect Red Sea, Persian Gulf, and Indian Ocean trade.
- Jain, Muslim, Hindu, and overseas merchants negotiate customs and protection.
- Enslavement, piracy, and commercial coercion accompany wealth.
## Act III — Mahmud Begada
- Campaigns against Junagadh and Champaner combine strategy, revenue, and royal image.
- Champaner-Pavagadh makes conquest visible through a new capital landscape.
- Later anecdotes about Begada's body require skepticism.
## Act IV — Portuguese pressure
- Portuguese naval power seeks cartaz control and Diu strongpoints.
- Gujarat, Mamluk, Ottoman, and local actors pursue distinct interests.
- The 1535–46 Diu struggles reshape but do not end Gujarati commerce.
## Act V — Mughal incorporation
- Bahadur Shah's death intensifies succession and noble conflict.
- Akbar takes Gujarat in 1572–73, then suppresses rebellion.
- Imperial roads and ports remain dependent on regional brokers.
## Subcontinental note
- Gujarat is both a subcontinental crossroads and an Indian Ocean polity.
- Sectarian affiliation mattered, but later communal nationalism cannot explain its coalitions.
## How we know
- Arabic, Persian, Jain, and Portuguese texts offer interested perspectives.
- Mosques, stepwells, temples, coins, and port archaeology reveal patronage and exchange.
- Merchant records survive unevenly and underrepresent labor.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Gujarat's architecture proves frictionless “synthesis.”
- Do not claim Portuguese arrival immediately displaced Asian trade.
- Do not claim conquest narratives transparently report conversions.
- Do not claim Mughal victory erased regional power.
