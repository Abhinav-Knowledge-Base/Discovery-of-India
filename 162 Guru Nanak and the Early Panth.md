## Before
- Punjab contains Sufi, Nath, Vaishnava, Islamic, and occupational institutions.
- Lodi and early Mughal power is uneven across towns and villages.
## The turn
- Nanak teaches one divine reality, truthful living, remembrance, and critique of hollow status.
- Kartarpur joins household, cultivation, congregation, and song.
## After
- Successive Gurus develop centers, script, offices, and a growing canon.
- The *Adi Granth* in 1604 defines authority without closing future Sikh history.
## Act I — Nanak in context
- Use Janamsakhis as theological biographies, not literal travel logs.
- His vocabulary engages multiple traditions without being reducible to any.
- “Neither Hindu nor Muslim” requires contextual, textual caution.
## Act II — Word and music
- Shabad is performed in prescribed ragas and congregational settings.
- Mardana traditions foreground Muslim musician participation and later memory.
- Oral performance and writing work together.
## Act III — Kartarpur
- Langar institutionalizes food, service, donation, and discipline.
- Shared eating challenges rank but does not prove caste vanished.
- Nanak's appointment of Angad establishes chosen succession over heredity.
## Act IV — Building the panth
- Gurmukhi standardization, Goindwal, manjis, and Amritsar expand organization.
- Women participate in labor and congregation, while records remain male-centered.
- Patronage and offerings create resources as well as disputes.
## Act V — Scripture and sovereignty
- Guru Arjan compiles writings of Sikh Gurus and selected bhagats.
- Harmandir Sahib becomes a central institutional site.
- His execution under Jahangir must be read through fiscal, political, and religious evidence.
## Subcontinental note
- Early Sikh distinctiveness develops historically; it is neither timeless sameness nor sudden separation.
- Sectarian identity here is not equivalent to later communal nationalism.
## How we know
- Guru compositions and early manuscripts anchor teaching and canon formation.
- Janamsakhis vary by recension and community.
- Mughal texts, inscriptions, material sites, and rahit traditions provide partial external checks.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Janamsakhi journeys are all verifiable itineraries.
- Do not claim langar permanently erased caste.
- Do not claim Sikh identity appeared complete in 1469.
- Do not explain Guru Arjan's execution through one motive alone.
