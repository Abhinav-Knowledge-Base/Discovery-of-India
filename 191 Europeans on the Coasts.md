## Before

Ocean trade already linked Gujarati, Arab, Persian, African, Malay, Tamil, and Chinese merchants through established credit.

## The turn

Portuguese violence was followed by Dutch, English, French, and Danish companies bargaining with Indian rulers and merchants.

## After

Fortified settlements and armies mattered, but inland revenue and Indian allies remained decisive.

## Act I - Portuguese intervention

Naval guns, forts, missions, and passes disrupted routes without complete monopoly.

## Act II - Company pluralism

European companies competed and depended on Asian brokers and producers.

## Act III - Port societies

Goa, Surat, Madras, Pondicherry, Cochin, and Tranquebar mixed law, conversion, slavery, and exchange.

## Act IV - Textiles and labor

Weavers, dyers, dubashes, sailors, fishers, and enslaved people enabled trade.

## Act V - Warehouse to fort

European wars became coastal wars, but conquest required inland fiscal access.

## Subcontinental note

Coasts connected the subcontinent to Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, the Gulf, East Africa, and Europe; Pakistan and Bangladesh ports share this history.

## How we know

Factory diaries, customs, shipwrecks, maps, mission texts, merchant letters, textiles, and architecture show contact and coercion.

## Do not claim

- Europeans introduced long-distance trade to India.
- All companies had identical capacities.
- Coastal communities were passive.
- Fortified ports made conquest inevitable.
