## Before
The series begins before historical names and borders. Landscapes, tools, food and bodies—not later national memories—set the scale.

## The turn
Stone technologies and changing habitats reveal many deep histories before named polities. The camera centers labor, food, gender and environment, then labels the move from observation to inference.

## After
Consequences remain regional rather than uniform. Open questions pass to [Ep 2 — The Narmada Witness](02%20The%20Narmada%20Witness.md), where a different archive changes what can responsibly be said.

## Subcontinental note
The frame is the historical subcontinent, not modern-border nationalism. Pakistan, India and Afghanistan enter where sites, rivers and routes connect them; local agency and regional difference remain visible.

## How we know
The episode combines archaeological contexts, environmental proxies and, where relevant, textual, epigraphic or biological evidence. Sparse fossils, disturbed deposits and broad dates keep routes and makers uncertain. Evidence, inference and later memory receive separate labels.

## Do not claim
- Do not claim that modern India existed then.
- Do not claim that one tool names one people.
- Do not claim that one migration explains settlement.
- Do not claim that climate alone determined change.
