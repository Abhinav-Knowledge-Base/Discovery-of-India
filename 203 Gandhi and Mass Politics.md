## Before

Indian nationalism already included constitutionalists, revolutionaries, workers, peasants, diaspora networks, and community organizations.

## The turn

Champaran, non-cooperation, salt satyagraha, and Quit India translated local grievances into campaigns through ashrams, volunteers, symbols, and negotiation.

## After

Mass politics delegitimized empire but neither prevented Partition nor resolved disputes over caste, minority safeguards, class, and centralized leadership.

## Act I - Experiments and organization

Gandhi's South African experience, ashrams, vows, and newspapers shaped a demanding political method.

## Act II - Non-cooperation

Khilafat alliance widened participation but Chauri Chaura, repression, and communal tensions exposed limits.

## Act III - Salt and civil disobedience

The march dramatized monopoly while regional campaigns gave disobedience different meanings.

## Act IV - Caste and gender disputes

Ambedkar challenged Gandhi on representation; women entered public struggle without automatic equality.

## Act V - Quit India and last years

Leader arrests produced decentralized revolt; Gandhi then confronted communal killing and displacement.

## Subcontinental note

Gandhian campaigns varied in Kerala, Tamil regions, Andhra, Bengal, Punjab, the Northwest Frontier, Assam, and princely states.

## How we know

Collected writings, newspapers, police reports, ashram rules, photographs, film, participant memoirs, and critics' texts permit no single authorized Gandhi.

## Do not claim

- Gandhi single-handedly won independence.
- Nonviolence meant no coercion or violence occurred.
- Harijan service equaled Ambedkar's anti-caste politics.
- Women gained equal leadership through participation.
