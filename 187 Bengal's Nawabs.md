## Before

Textiles, agrarian expansion, ports, bankers, and revenue administration connected inland producers to ocean trade.

## The turn

Murshid Quli Khan's successors consolidated rule while European companies remained licensed merchants among many actors.

## After

Plassey and Buxar grew from conspiracies and alliances; the 1765 diwani gave the Company fiscal power.

## Act I - Revenue capital

Murshidabad joined court, zamindars, bankers, Armenian merchants, and textile districts.

## Act II - Regulated commerce

European and Indian traders competed within customs rules they also evaded.

## Act III - Siraj's crisis

Court factions, bankers, commanders, and Company officers pursued divergent interests.

## Act IV - Mir Qasim's challenge

Reform exposed conflict between territorial government and Company privilege.

## Act V - Remembering conquest

Imperial stories converted contingent fiscal capture into British destiny.

## Subcontinental note

Bengal included present Bangladesh, West Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha connections; river and production zones crossed later borders.

## How we know

Chronicles, consultations, banking records, textiles, coins, petitions, and archaeology expose interested narratives.

## Do not claim

- Plassey was won by battlefield superiority alone.
- Siraj was only a caricatured despot.
- Merchants acted as one bloc.
- Company rule instantly replaced nawabi institutions.
