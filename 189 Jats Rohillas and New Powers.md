## Before

The countryside contained armed zamindars, peasant communities, pastoral routes, market towns, and mobile soldiers.

## The turn

Rebellion and service produced Bharatpur, Rohilkhand, and regimes negotiating with Mughals, Marathas, Afghans, and Awadh.

## After

Coalition wars and fiscal pressures transformed these powers, later simplified as disorder before colonial rule.

## Act I - Jat mobilization

Communities near Mathura turned cultivation, fortification, and rebellion into authority.

## Act II - Bharatpur state

Suraj Mal used diplomacy and revenue expertise beyond one caste constituency.

## Act III - Rohilla networks

Afghan military migrants established Rohilkhand amid a diverse population.

## Act IV - Gangetic war

Awadh, Maratha, Mughal, Afghan, and Company interventions redrew obligations.

## Act V - Local archives

Forts, genealogies, revenue papers, and memory contest imperial narratives.

## Subcontinental note

Braj, Rohilkhand, Haryana, Rajasthan, and the upper Gangetic plain were overlapping zones, not sealed ethnic territories.

## How we know

Persian histories, Braj texts, genealogies, surveys, fort archaeology, coins, and village traditions are partial and political.

## Do not claim

- Jat or Rohilla identity was timeless and uniform.
- Agrarian origin made these states egalitarian.
- New powers filled an empty vacuum.
- Colonial martial labels are neutral.
