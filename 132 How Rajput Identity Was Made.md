## Act 1 — The crowded field
Before the episode's turn, authority in this region was divided among courts, local chiefs, corporate bodies, and communities managing land, water, labor, and routes. Many pastoral, tribal, peasant, and ruling groups entered or were excluded from changing status claims.

## Act 2 — The historical turn
Rajput identity emerged over centuries as lineages, warriors, bards, marriage networks, and states made ranked claims rather than preserving one ancient race. The film distinguishes a raid for movable wealth, conquest in battle, annexation through continuing rule, temple patronage, and temple plunder.

## Act 3 — Material power
Inscriptions, genealogies, bardic texts, forts, hero stones, marriage records, and clan landscapes show how sovereignty depended on artisans, accountants, transport, food, animals, water, and maintained infrastructure.

## Act 4 — Lives inside the claim
Follow peasants facing dues and ecological risk; artisans and merchants negotiating demand; women exercising constrained agency; enslaved people and captives enduring coercion; and forest, pastoral, coastal, or frontier communities shaping routes.

## Act 5 — Limits and afterlives
Afterward, the new settlement remained uneven: military success had to become tribute, garrisons, appointments, revenue, patronage, or negotiated local compliance. Later memory selected heroes and disasters that contemporary evidence does not always support.

## Subcontinental note
Many pastoral, tribal, peasant, and ruling groups entered or were excluded from changing status claims. Political simultaneity matters: no dynasty occupied the whole subcontinent, and developments elsewhere were not merely reactions to Delhi or the featured court.

## How we know
Inscriptions, genealogies, bardic texts, forts, hero stones, marriage records, and clan landscapes must be cross-read because inscriptions praise donors, chronicles shape moral narratives, monuments change over time, and archaeology preserves unevenly. Absence from elite records is not evidence that poorer, mobile, female, or enslaved people were absent.

## Do not claim
- Rajputs were not one biologically continuous ancient people with fixed customs.
- A victory inscription or court chronicle proves uniform, permanent control over every place it names.
- Raid, conquest, annexation, temple patronage, and temple plunder were interchangeable acts.
- Monumental patronage reveals the beliefs or benefits of every subject.
- The conflict can be reduced to an inevitable Hindu-versus-Muslim civilizational struggle.

Rajasthan; Gujarat; Malwa; Bundelkhand; Himalayan foothills. Pair legible inscriptions, coins, working surfaces, water systems, roads, ports, and settlement context with monuments; avoid generic spectacle.
