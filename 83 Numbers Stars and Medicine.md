# Ep 83 — Numbers, Stars, and Medicine

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Astronomers, calculators, and physicians refine mobile knowledge traditions whose later fame can obscure uncertainty, collaboration, and transmission. |
| **Years** | c. 300–650 CE |
| **Before–after** | Earlier siddhanta and medical traditions → works associated with Aryabhata, Varahamihira, and Vagbhata |

## Before
Numerical notation, calendrics, surgery, pharmacology, and astral sciences had long histories across South Asia and exchanges with Hellenistic and other worlds.

## The turn
Authors synthesize observation, algorithms, textual authority, and debate into compact Sanskrit works copied and commented upon for centuries.

## After
Methods travel through regional schools, Arabic translations, and later commentaries; attribution and priority become modern nationalist battlegrounds.

## Act I — Reading a manuscript backward
Begin with a late copy to show the gulf between an authorial date and the physical witness that survives.

## Act II — Aryabhata’s rotating Earth
Demonstrate place value, sine tables, eclipse calculation, and Earth’s rotation without importing modern scientific categories wholesale.

## Act III — Varahamihira’s compilation
Use the *Pancasiddhantika* to reveal multiple astronomical lineages and intellectual exchange rather than isolated genius.

## Act IV — Bodies and remedies
Treat medical compendia as learned traditions alongside household, artisanal, pastoral, and forest pharmacological knowledge.

## Act V — How knowledge moves
Follow teachers, scribes, commentators, courts, monasteries, and translators; include exclusions tied to language, caste, and gender.

## Subcontinental note
Knowledge crossed present borders among Sri Lanka, Nepal, Central Asia, and western Asia; circulation did not erase distinct institutions or ownership.

## How we know
Manuscript recensions, commentaries, inscriptions, instruments, and later translations preserve ideas but seldom reconstruct laboratories or biographies securely.

## Do not claim
- Do not say one individual “invented zero” in a single documented moment.
- Do not label ancient concepts identical to modern astronomy or medicine.
- Do not deny Greek, Iranian, Central Asian, or later Arabic exchanges.
- Do not present prescriptive medical texts as universal access to care.
- Do not claim textual silence proves women or non-elites lacked knowledge.
