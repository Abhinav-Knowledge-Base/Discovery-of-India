## Before

Shivaji's kingdom left forts, offices, and a language of sovereignty contested after Aurangzeb's death.

## The turn

Peshwas and the Sindhia, Holkar, Gaekwad, and Bhonsle houses expanded through chauth, warfare, service, and brokerage.

## After

Confederal rivalry and Company alliances helped produce defeats ending Peshwa rule in 1818, though regional houses endured.

## Act I - Peshwa ascendancy

Shahu's settlement shifted practical leadership toward Pune.

## Act II - Tribute networks

Chauth depended on negotiation with local rulers and revenue intermediaries.

## Act III - Many centers

Gwalior, Indore, Baroda, and Nagpur developed distinct courts and interests.

## Act IV - Panipat and recovery

The 1761 defeat was catastrophic but did not end Maratha power.

## Act V - Company wars

Three wars and Indian alliances progressively constrained the confederacy.

## Subcontinental note

Campaigns linked Maharashtra to Gujarat, Malwa, Delhi, Odisha, and the south, with uneven effects on cultivators and towns.

## How we know

Bakhars, Persian chronicles, treaties, letters, revenue records, forts, and grants permit competing reconstructions.

## Do not claim

- Marathas formed a centralized nation-state.
- Panipat permanently destroyed Maratha power.
- Expansion was either pure liberation or pure plunder.
- British victory was militarily inevitable.
