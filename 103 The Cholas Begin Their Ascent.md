# Ep 103 — The Cholas Begin Their Ascent

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Vijayalaya and Aditya I turn a Kaveri delta lineage into an expanding state through war, irrigation, temples, and alliances. |
| **Years** | c. 850–985 CE |
| **Before–after** | Muttaraiyar and Pallava–Pandya competition → foundations of imperial Chola power |

## Before
The Kaveri delta contained old Chola memories, Muttaraiyar chiefs, cultivators, merchants, temples, Jain communities, and layered water management.

## The turn
Vijayalaya captures Thanjavur; Aditya I defeats Aparajita; Parantaka I expands before Rashtrakuta victory checks Chola claims.

## After
Succession crises and recovery lead to Rajaraja I, but imperial scale is not inevitable from the ninth-century beginning.

## Act I — An old name, a new house
Distinguish Sangam Cholas from Vijayalaya’s lineage and ask how genealogy manufactures continuity.

## Act II — Capturing the delta
Connect Thanjavur, channels, labor, paddy, flood risk, local chiefs, and temples rather than attributing wealth only to kings.

## Act III — Stone and copper
Early temples and Anbil plates show patronage and memory, while inscriptions privilege landed and ritual elites.

## Act IV — Victory checked
Pair Parantaka’s Madurai claims with Takkolam defeat and Sri Lankan sources, preserving rival perspectives.

## Act V — Institutions before empire
Study *ur*, *sabha*, *nagaram*, taxes, caste exclusions, women donors, merchants, and agrarian expansion without idealizing self-government.

## Subcontinental note
Chola ties to Sri Lanka, Kerala, Karnataka, and Andhra involved distinct states and communities; later conquest should not colonize their earlier histories.

## How we know
Temple inscriptions, copper plates, hero stones, architecture, coins, and rival Rashtrakuta and Sri Lankan records grow denser but remain partisan.

## Do not claim
- Do not make Vijayalaya a securely direct descendant of Sangam kings.
- Do not call Kaveri fertility a natural gift independent of labor.
- Do not describe assemblies as universal democracy.
- Do not map victory titles as continuous territorial rule.
- Do not treat imperial Chola expansion as inevitable.
