# Ep 106 — Assam and the Northeast Before Ahom

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | After ancient Kamarupa, valley kingdoms, hill societies, routes, and ritual centers shape a Northeast whose history does not begin with the Ahoms. |
| **Years** | c. 750–1228 CE |
| **Before–after** | Salastambha Kamarupa → Pala-Kamarupa, Kamata, Chutiya, Kachari, and other formations |

## Before
The Brahmaputra and Barak valleys, hills, forests, and eastern routes supported wet-rice, shifting cultivation, pastoralism, crafts, and trade.

## The turn
Kamarupa’s later dynasties fragment and recombine as new centers and communities become visible before the Tai Ahom arrival.

## After
From 1228, Ahom state formation joins rather than inaugurates a crowded political landscape.

## Act I — Beyond one valley kingdom
Track Kamarupa’s contraction without treating all northeastern peoples as former subjects of a single state.

## Act II — Sources with uneven light
Combine grants, archaeology, oral traditions, linguistics, and later chronicles while refusing to force them into one royal chronology.

## Act III — Hills and valleys
Show exchange in salt, iron, elephants, forest products, textiles, and crops alongside conflict and ecological knowledge.

## Act IV — New centers
Introduce Kamata, Dimasa-Kachari, Chutiya, and other formations with cautious dates and names derived from later records.

## Act V — Before 1228
End at a meeting of Tai migrants and established societies, not a “discovery” of empty land.

## Subcontinental note
The Northeast connects to Bengal, Bhutan, Tibet, Myanmar, and Yunnan through its own peoples and routes; it is not an appendage or sealed frontier.

## How we know
Inscriptions thin after Kamarupa; archaeology, place names, linguistics, oral histories, and later *buranjis* require different standards of inference.

## Do not claim
- Do not make the Northeast’s history begin with Ahom arrival.
- Do not extend Kamarupa sovereignty over every hill society.
- Do not translate oral tradition directly into exact dates.
- Do not assign modern ethnic labels unchanged to early communities.
- Do not portray shifting cultivation or forests as backward emptiness.
