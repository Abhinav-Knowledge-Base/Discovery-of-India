# Ep 78 — The Kadambas and Early Karnataka

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | From Banavasi, Kadamba rulers negotiate Sanskrit prestige, emerging Kannada expression, agrarian frontiers, and western-coast routes. |
| **Years** | c. 325–550 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-Satavahana Karnataka polities → Kadamba, Ganga, and early Chalukya regional orders |

## Before
Karnataka contained megalithic inheritances, farming and forest communities, Jain and Brahmanical networks, ports, and local chiefs beyond northern court narratives.

## The turn
Mayurasharma’s lineage establishes authority around Banavasi, while inscriptions translate conflict, sacrifice, grants, and genealogy into legitimacy.

## After
Kadamba branches and practices survive under changing overlords as Chalukyas become more prominent.

## Act I — Talagunda speaks
Read the pillar inscription’s origin story as political memory composed generations after Mayurasharma.

## Act II — Banavasi and the coast
Follow routes between uplands, forests, pepper zones, ports, monasteries, and inland courts.

## Act III — Languages of rule
Compare Sanskrit records with the Halmidi Kannada inscription without declaring a single birthday for Kannada identity.

## Act IV — Gifts and fields
Examine grants as negotiated claims over revenue, cultivation, labor, and ritual status, not a simple feudal template.

## Act V — Parallel Karnatakas
Place Kadambas beside Western Gangas, Alupas, Pallavas, local communities, and mobile traders.

## Subcontinental note
Early Karnataka was connected to Tamilakam, the Deccan, Konkan, and Indian Ocean traffic; it was neither isolated nor a province of a northern classical age.

## How we know
Talagunda, Gudnapur, and Halmidi inscriptions, coins, temples, ceramics, and later traditions differ in date and purpose.

## Do not claim
- Do not treat Mayurasharma’s Brahmin-to-warrior story as unmediated biography.
- Do not call Halmidi the uncontested first Kannada inscription without qualification.
- Do not make modern state borders ancient political boundaries.
- Do not infer all caste practice from royal genealogical claims.
- Do not describe forests as empty land awaiting royal cultivation.
