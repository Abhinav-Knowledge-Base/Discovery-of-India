## Before

Precolonial claims to land and produce overlapped among cultivators, communities, zamindars, chiefs, temples, and states.

## The turn

Permanent, ryotwari, and mahalwari settlements translated varied rights into taxable categories suited to regional Company strategies.

## After

Debt, commercialization, dispossession, migration, litigation, and protest transformed rural society without producing one uniform outcome.

## Act I - Permanent Settlement

Bengal zamindars became fixed-revenue proprietors while cultivators' rights remained contested.

## Act II - Ryotwari

Madras and Bombay officials assessed cultivators directly in theory but relied on surveys and village power.

## Act III - Mahalwari

North Indian village bodies became revenue units through changing estimates and intermediaries.

## Act IV - Credit and crops

Moneylenders, merchants, indigo, cotton, and opium connected households to unstable markets.

## Act V - Resistance and adaptation

Petitions, evasion, flight, legal claims, and revolt show rural people making constrained choices.

## Subcontinental note

Punjab, Bengal, western and southern India, and tribal frontiers received different settlements; regional ecologies shaped their effects.

## How we know

Settlement reports, cadastral maps, account books, petitions, vernacular testimony, and household objects reveal official categories and lived practice.

## Do not claim

- One settlement covered all India.
- Private property simply modernized agriculture.
- Every moneylender or zamindar had identical interests.
- Peasants only reacted and never negotiated.
