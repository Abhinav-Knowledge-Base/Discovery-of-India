## Before

Dalit, non-Brahman, Adivasi, and oppressed-caste communities had long resisted exclusion through religion, labor, education, and organization.

## The turn

Mass associations, print, satyagraha, electoral bargaining, conversion, and Self-Respect campaigns confronted caste in state and society.

## After

Constitutional rights and reservations changed institutions, but violence, landlessness, patriarchy, and graded inequality persisted.

## Act I - Ambedkar's public politics

Mahad, temple-entry campaigns, labor work, and testimony linked civic equality to economic power.

## Act II - Representation dispute

Separate electorates, the Poona Pact, and reservations exposed conflict over who could speak for Depressed Classes.

## Act III - Periyar and Self-Respect

Tamil campaigns attacked Brahmanism, patriarchy, imposed Hindi, and ritual hierarchy.

## Act IV - Women and labor

Anti-caste women wrote, organized, and challenged domination inside communities as well as outside them.

## Act V - Conversion and constitutionalism

Ambedkar's Buddhism and legal work pursued fraternity while warning against social democracy's absence.

## Subcontinental note

Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu were key centers, but Adi movements, Namashudras, Ezhavas, Dalit Christians, and regional parties created wider histories.

## How we know

Speeches, newspapers, organization records, police files, oral histories, statues, pamphlets, and autobiographies preserve debate and later appropriation.

## Do not claim

- One leader represents all oppressed castes.
- Reservation was Ambedkar's only concern.
- Periyar's movement had no internal gender or caste tensions.
- Legal equality automatically annihilated caste.
