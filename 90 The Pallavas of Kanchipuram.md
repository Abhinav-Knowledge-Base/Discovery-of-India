# Ep 90 — The Pallavas of Kanchipuram

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Pallava Kanchipuram meets Chalukya armies and turns stone, Sanskrit, Tamil devotion, and maritime links into competing visions of authority. |
| **Years** | c. 575–730 CE |
| **Before–after** | Early Pallava consolidation → monumental Kanchipuram and Mamallapuram |

## Before
Pallava authority shared northern Tamilakam with local chiefs, Jain and Buddhist centers, Brahmanical institutions, merchants, and agrarian communities.

## The turn
Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I fight the Badami Chalukyas while sponsoring rock-cut monuments and a newly assertive court image.

## After
Rajasimha’s structural temples deepen Kanchi’s monumental landscape as renewed warfare and succession pressures continue.

## Act I — Kanchi, many cities
Reconstruct a multi-religious center from scattered archaeology rather than making surviving temples stand for the whole city.

## Act II — King in a cave
Mahendravarman’s inscriptions and farce stage wit, sectarian rivalry, and royal persona, not a simple conversion biography.

## Act III — Vatapi and Mamalla
Cross-cut Narasimhavarman’s victory with Chalukya recovery so neither dynasty monopolizes the century.

## Act IV — Making Mamallapuram
Follow quarrying, artisans, unfinished monuments, water, merchants, and port debates behind spectacular royal labels.

## Act V — Devotion and society
Set Alvar and Nayanmar compositions beside Jain, Buddhist, local, caste, gender, and agrarian histories that devotional canons only partly reveal.

## Subcontinental note
Pallava links with Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia were diplomatic, commercial, and religious connections among distinct societies, not a “Greater India” colonization.

## How we know
Inscriptions, monuments, devotional texts compiled later, archaeology, and rival prashastis leave port functions and event chronology debated.

## Do not claim
- Do not call Mamallapuram’s monuments products of one ruler with certainty.
- Do not treat unfinished “rathas” as wheeled chariots or ordinary temples.
- Do not frame Chalukya–Pallava war as a modern linguistic conflict.
- Do not claim bhakti erased caste or gender hierarchy.
- Do not describe Southeast Asian influence as unilateral Indianization.
