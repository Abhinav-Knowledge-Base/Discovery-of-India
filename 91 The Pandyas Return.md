# Ep 91 — The Pandyas Return

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Pandya rulers reclaim an ancient name from Madurai, drawing power from agrarian interiors, pearl coasts, temples, and war. |
| **Years** | c. 560–920 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-Sangam transformation → first Pandya empire and Chola conquest |

## Before
Southern Tamilakam contained chiefs, cultivators, pastoralists, pearl fishers, merchants, Jain and Buddhist institutions, and expanding Brahmanical networks.

## The turn
Kadungon and successors style themselves restorers, compete with Pallavas and Chalukyas, and link Madurai to the Gulf of Mannar.

## After
Pandya power contracts under Chola expansion, but dynastic claims and regional institutions persist toward a later resurgence.

## Act I — The rhetoric of return
Question Velvikudi’s later account of Kalabhra seizure and Pandya restoration as a charter’s interested memory.

## Act II — Madurai and countryside
Connect court, tanks, fields, pastoral tracts, temple centers, and communities whose labor underwrote royal gifts.

## Act III — Pearl coast
Follow Parathavar and other fishing and trading communities across the Gulf without reducing them to royal revenue.

## Act IV — Faiths in contention
Use hagiographies cautiously when discussing Shaiva expansion, Jain presence, Vaishnava devotion, caste, and women devotees.

## Act V — Fighting on many fronts
Map variable campaigns against Pallavas, Cheras, Sri Lankan kingdoms, and local chiefs rather than one stable empire.

## Subcontinental note
The Palk Strait and Gulf of Mannar joined Pandya and Sri Lankan histories through trade and conflict while preserving separate political worlds.

## How we know
Copper plates, cave and temple inscriptions, coins, archaeology, hagiographies, and Sri Lankan chronicles offer partisan and differently dated evidence.

## Do not claim
- Do not accept “Pandya restoration” language as neutral history.
- Do not portray Jain decline as one sudden royal persecution.
- Do not annex northern Sri Lanka to Pandya history through campaign claims.
- Do not treat pearl divers as a timeless single caste.
- Do not infer uniform agrarian control from grants.

Film Madurai, Vaigai irrigation, Kalugumalai, coastal fisheries, charter plates, and coinage; distinguish hagiographic scenes from inscriptions.
