## Act 1 — The crowded field
Before the episode's turn, authority in this region was divided among courts, local chiefs, corporate bodies, and communities managing land, water, labor, and routes. Pandyas, Hoysalas, Kerala ports, Sri Lanka, Vijayanagara, and Indian Ocean merchants remained active.

## Act 2 — The historical turn
Ma'bar governors broke from Delhi and built a short-lived sultanate amid Pandya succession, Hoysala intervention, trade, and local resistance. The film distinguishes a raid for movable wealth, conquest in battle, annexation through continuing rule, temple patronage, and temple plunder.

## Act 3 — Material power
Coins, inscriptions, Ibn Battuta, temple records, Madurai archaeology, ports, and later chronicles show how sovereignty depended on artisans, accountants, transport, food, animals, water, and maintained infrastructure.

## Act 4 — Lives inside the claim
Follow peasants facing dues and ecological risk; artisans and merchants negotiating demand; women exercising constrained agency; enslaved people and captives enduring coercion; and forest, pastoral, coastal, or frontier communities shaping routes.

## Act 5 — Limits and afterlives
Afterward, the new settlement remained uneven: military success had to become tribute, garrisons, appointments, revenue, patronage, or negotiated local compliance. Later memory selected heroes and disasters that contemporary evidence does not always support.

## Subcontinental note
Pandyas, Hoysalas, Kerala ports, Sri Lanka, Vijayanagara, and Indian Ocean merchants remained active. Political simultaneity matters: no dynasty occupied the whole subcontinent, and developments elsewhere were not merely reactions to Delhi or the featured court.

## How we know
Coins, inscriptions, Ibn Battuta, temple records, Madurai archaeology, ports, and later chronicles must be cross-read because inscriptions praise donors, chronicles shape moral narratives, monuments change over time, and archaeology preserves unevenly. Absence from elite records is not evidence that poorer, mobile, female, or enslaved people were absent.

## Do not claim
- The sultanate was not merely an interlude of religious destruction; rule, war, taxation, patronage, and plunder must be distinguished.
- A victory inscription or court chronicle proves uniform, permanent control over every place it names.
- Raid, conquest, annexation, temple patronage, and temple plunder were interchangeable acts.
- Monumental patronage reveals the beliefs or benefits of every subject.
- The conflict can be reduced to an inevitable Hindu-versus-Muslim civilizational struggle.
