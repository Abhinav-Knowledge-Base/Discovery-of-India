## Before

Forest, hill, and plateau societies maintained diverse cultivation, hunting, pastoral, sacred, and chiefly systems linked to wider markets.

## The turn

Revenue expansion, private claims, forest law, labor recruitment, and outsider settlement intensified pressure and provoked regionally distinct uprisings.

## After

Rebellions forced administrative exceptions but colonial rule continued to classify, police, extract, and restrict Adivasi worlds.

## Act I - Many regions, many causes

Bhil, Kol, Khasi, Santhal, Munda, Bastar, and Rampa struggles cannot be folded into one revolt.

## Act II - Santhal Hul

Sidhu, Kanhu, Phulo, Jhano, and thousands challenged zamindars, creditors, police, and Company rule.

## Act III - Ulgulan

Birsa Munda joined religious renewal to demands over khuntkatti land and forced labor.

## Act IV - Forest sovereignty

Reserved forests criminalized customary use and produced daily resistance beyond famous rebellions.

## Act V - Memory and politics

Songs, sacred places, martyrs, and later movements preserve histories often filtered through official files.

## Subcontinental note

Central India, Jharkhand, Odisha, Bengal, the Western Ghats, the Northeast, and Andhra hills have distinct peoples and political trajectories.

## How we know

Police reports and ethnography carry colonial bias; petitions, oral traditions, songs, archaeology, land records, and community scholarship correct the frame.

## Do not claim

- Adivasi names describe one homogeneous people.
- Every uprising sought modern nationalism.
- Isolation kept communities outside markets and politics.
- Colonial ethnography is transparent fact.
