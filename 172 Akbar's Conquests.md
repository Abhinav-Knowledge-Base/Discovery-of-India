## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Akbar survives regency and rebellion, then builds an empire through siege, alliance, mobility, and relentless incorporation. |
| **Years** | 1556–1605 |
| **Before–after** | From a restored Delhi–Punjab foothold to the largest Mughal claim yet. |
## Before
- Humayun's restoration remains vulnerable to Sur and regional rivals.
- Thirteen-year-old Akbar depends on Bairam Khan and military households.
## The turn
- Second Panipat defeats Hemu's coalition in 1556.
- Malwa, Gondwana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Bengal, Kabul, Kashmir, Sind, and Deccan campaigns follow.
## After
- Provincial institutions and mobile court bind conquests imperfectly.
- Jahangir inherits both broad claims and unresolved frontier wars.
## Act I — Regency and autonomy
- Bairam Khan leads recovery before Akbar dismisses him in 1560.
- Court women and foster relations shape early factional politics.
- Hemu's rise demonstrates non-dynastic opportunity within Sur conflict.
## Act II — Central India
- Malwa's invasion brings plunder and abuses under Adham Khan.
- Rani Durgavati resists Mughal expansion in Garha-Katanga.
- Incorporation varies among annexation, tribute, and local autonomy.
## Act III — Rajasthan
- Amber's alliance opens dynastic service and marriage networks.
- Chittor's siege ends in massacre commemorated through competing narratives.
- Mewar resistance continues under Maharana Pratap after Haldighati.
## Act IV — Gujarat and Bengal
- Rapid Gujarat conquest depends on ports, nobles, and repeated suppression.
- Bengal campaigns confront Afghan chiefs and riverine logistics.
- Victory dates conceal years of unstable command.
## Act V — Northwest and Deccan
- Kabul, Kashmir, Sind, and Kandahar extend strategic reach.
- Deccan intervention begins against Ahmadnagar and other sultanates.
- Akbar's empire remains mobile, negotiated, and militarized.
## Subcontinental note
- Imperial integration creates careers and violence simultaneously.
- Rajput, Afghan, Gond, and Deccan actors make strategic choices, not predetermined communal responses.
## How we know
- Abu'l Fazl rationalizes conquest as just sovereignty.
- Badauni, regional chronicles, inscriptions, paintings, forts, and revenue records dissent or qualify.
- Battle numbers and speeches are literary constructions unless corroborated.
## Do not claim
- Do not call all alliances voluntary partnership.
- Do not call every resistance Hindu opposition.
- Do not minimize Chittor's documented mass killing.
- Do not equate conquest maps with uniform control.
