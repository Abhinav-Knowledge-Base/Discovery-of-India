## Before

Late colonial India contained interdependent provinces, princely states, minority politics, and movements divided over federation and representation.

## The turn

The Second World War, Lahore Resolution, Quit India, INA, elections, Cabinet Mission, and failed power-sharing accelerated a rushed transfer.

## After

India and Pakistan emerged amid killings and migration; Bengal, Punjab, Sindh, Kashmir, and refugee lives followed different trajectories.

## Act I - War and scarcity

Recruitment, military economy, the Bengal famine, INA, and demobilization changed political possibilities.

## Act II - Negotiating sovereignty

Congress, Muslim League, Sikh leaders, Scheduled Caste representatives, and others disputed federation and safeguards.

## Act III - Punjab and Bengal

Boundary awards cut villages, canals, railways, cities, and kin networks amid organized and retaliatory violence.

## Act IV - Sindh and Kashmir

Sindhi Hindu displacement unfolded differently; Kashmir's accession, invasion, war, and UN process opened an enduring dispute.

## Act V - Refuge, gender, memory

Abduction, recovery laws, camps, property claims, silence, and rebuilding shaped lives beyond official dates.

## Subcontinental note

Partition history must retain Pakistan and Bangladesh links: East Bengal migration continued for decades, and 1971 reshaped earlier refugee worlds.

## How we know

Cabinet papers, League and Congress records, boundary maps, police files, refugee claims, women's testimony, oral history, film, and family objects conflict.

## Do not claim

- Partition was ancient communal hatred made inevitable.
- Punjab's experience represents Bengal or Sindh.
- All migration occurred in 1947.
- Women's recovery policies simply restored agency.
