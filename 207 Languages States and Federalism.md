## Before

Colonial provinces and princely mergers cut across linguistic regions, while nationalist leaders had earlier organized Congress on language lines.

## The turn

Potti Sriramulu's death and Andhra's creation accelerated the States Reorganisation Commission and the 1956 linguistic settlement.

## After

New states, official-language compromises, and regional parties stabilized some conflicts while autonomy movements and border disputes persisted.

## Act I - Andhra catalyst

A long Telugu movement, not one fast alone, made linguistic statehood unavoidable.

## Act II - Reorganisation Commission

Evidence balanced language, administration, economy, security, and minority concerns without a perfect map.

## Act III - Hindi and resistance

Official-language policy provoked Tamil mobilization and produced continued use of English alongside Hindi.

## Act IV - Regional parties

DMK, Akali Dal, National Conference, and others made state power and coalition bargaining democratic arenas.

## Act V - Frontiers and new states

Maharashtra-Gujarat, Punjab-Haryana, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Manipur, and Tripura followed different paths.

## Subcontinental note

The Northeast combined linguistic, ethnic, tribal, security, and treaty histories; Kashmir and border regions cannot be reduced to language alone.

## How we know

Commission reports, parliamentary debates, movement pamphlets, songs, police files, election results, maps, and oral histories reveal competing federal claims.

## Do not claim

- Linguistic states inevitably Balkanized India.
- Each state contains one language.
- Hindi policy was settled in 1950.
- Regional parties are inherently anti-national.
