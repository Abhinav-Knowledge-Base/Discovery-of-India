## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Sher Shah turns Afghan coalition and eastern revenue into a short empire whose roads, currency, and administration are later magnified. |
| **Years** | 1530s–1556 |
| **Before–after** | From Bihar power-building to Sur fragmentation and Mughal restoration. |
## Before
- Afghan chiefs retain strong eastern bases after Lodi defeat.
- Bihar and Bengal provide revenue, forts, elephants, and competing elites.
## The turn
- Sher Khan defeats Humayun and becomes Sher Shah in 1540.
- Rapid conquest links Bengal, Delhi, Punjab, Malwa, and Rajasthan unevenly.
## After
- Islam Shah maintains authority through tighter military control.
- Succession conflict fractures Sur power after 1553.
## Act I — Farid becomes Sher
- Later biography turns service, family conflict, and tiger legend into destiny.
- Forts, marriage alliances, and Bengal campaigns explain ascent materially.
- Afghan identity contains rival lineages and interests.
## Act II — Conquering Humayun
- Chausa and Kannauj reward intelligence, preparation, and Mughal weakness.
- Victory creates a new regime, not an automatic administrative revolution.
- Sher Shah redistributes offices and lands to secure followers.
## Act III — Revenue and currency
- Survey, assessment, pattas, and qabuliats are documented unevenly.
- The silver rupiya supports exchange and royal credibility.
- Mughal adoption should not turn all later institutions into Sher Shah's invention.
## Act IV — Roads and forts
- Routes, sarais, trees, posts, and policing support armies, news, and commerce.
- Earlier roads are repaired and governed rather than created from nothing.
- Rohtas and Purana Qila materialize strategic control.
## Act V — Kalinjar and aftermath
- Sher Shah dies in a gunpowder accident during the Kalinjar siege.
- Islam Shah faces noble resistance despite centralizing efforts.
- Sur rivals enable Humayun's return.
## Subcontinental note
- The interregnum is a substantive Afghan imperial project, not a pause in Mughal history.
- Infrastructure benefits power and extraction as well as travelers.
## How we know
- Abbas Khan Sarwani writes under Akbar and idealizes Afghan governance.
- Coins, farmans, sarai inscriptions, roads, and forts test administrative claims.
- Later Mughal manuals may retrospectively systematize Sur practice.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Sher Shah invented the Grand Trunk Road.
- Do not claim his revenue system operated uniformly everywhere.
- Do not portray Afghans as one united faction.
- Do not treat brief duration as historical insignificance.
