## Act 1 — The crowded field
Before the episode's turn, authority in this region was divided among courts, local chiefs, corporate bodies, and communities managing land, water, labor, and routes. Bengal, Gujarat, Malwa, Jaunpur, Kashmir, Vijayanagara, and Bahmani power developed on their own trajectories.

## Act 2 — The historical turn
Rebellions, fiscal strain, succession, ecology, and Timur's invasion transformed Delhi from an imperial claimant into one power among regional states. The film distinguishes a raid for movable wealth, conquest in battle, annexation through continuing rule, temple patronage, and temple plunder.

## Act 3 — Material power
Chronicles, coins, abandoned capitals, famine references, inscriptions, regional archives, and Timur's accounts show how sovereignty depended on artisans, accountants, transport, food, animals, water, and maintained infrastructure.

## Act 4 — Lives inside the claim
Follow peasants facing dues and ecological risk; artisans and merchants negotiating demand; women exercising constrained agency; enslaved people and captives enduring coercion; and forest, pastoral, coastal, or frontier communities shaping routes.

## Act 5 — Limits and afterlives
Afterward, the new settlement remained uneven: military success had to become tribute, garrisons, appointments, revenue, patronage, or negotiated local compliance. Later memory selected heroes and disasters that contemporary evidence does not always support.

## Subcontinental note
Bengal, Gujarat, Malwa, Jaunpur, Kashmir, Vijayanagara, and Bahmani power developed on their own trajectories. Political simultaneity matters: no dynasty occupied the whole subcontinent, and developments elsewhere were not merely reactions to Delhi or the featured court.

## How we know
Chronicles, coins, abandoned capitals, famine references, inscriptions, regional archives, and Timur's accounts must be cross-read because inscriptions praise donors, chronicles shape moral narratives, monuments change over time, and archaeology preserves unevenly. Absence from elite records is not evidence that poorer, mobile, female, or enslaved people were absent.

## Do not claim
- Timur's 1398 sack was catastrophic but did not single-handedly cause every earlier or later fragmentation.
- A victory inscription or court chronicle proves uniform, permanent control over every place it names.
- Raid, conquest, annexation, temple patronage, and temple plunder were interchangeable acts.
- Monumental patronage reveals the beliefs or benefits of every subject.
- The conflict can be reduced to an inevitable Hindu-versus-Muslim civilizational struggle.
