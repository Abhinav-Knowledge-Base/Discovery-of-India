# Seasons and viewing paths

Every number below is checked against the file names in the [episode index](README.md). Cite episodes by number *and* title in syllabi.

Total: 18 seasons × 12 episodes = 216. Episodes 1–180 reach the eve of Company rule, so five-sixths of the series is devoted to the subcontinent before 1750.

## Recap and continuity rules

1. Episode recaps last no more than 75 seconds and answer “what changed,” not “who won.”
2. Season premieres may use a three-minute bridge locating all parallel regional lanes.
3. Recaps never reuse violence as spectacle or restage an uncertain event as fact.
4. A carried term returns with its changed meaning; it is not silently treated as stable.
5. Maps resume from the previous episode's final dated layer, then clear unsupported claims.
6. Each finale leaves two open questions and names the evidence available in the next season.
7. “Previously” sequences identify episode numbers for classroom citation.
8. Post-1947 recaps retain Pakistan and Bangladesh connections when causal or comparative, not as token updates.

## Thematic paths

### States, law and sovereignty
36–37, 42–43, 49, 51–52, 60, 67, 73–74, 85, 89, 97, 99–100, 109, 111, 133–134, 137–139, 145, 147, 171, 173, 181–182, 192, 196, 205–207.
Use this path to compare forms of the state, not as a relay race of dynasties. Add 42 (the Vrijji Confederacy), 59 (Life Beyond the Imperial Core) and 194 (Adivasi Resistance) for people living beyond or against the state.

### Caste and social rank
35, 44, 75, 96, 112, 158, 160–161, 165, 193, 199, 201, 212.
This path keeps apart prescriptive texts, local practice, occupational hierarchy, anti-caste thought, census counting, law, and democratic politics.

### Gender, household and sexuality
6, 19, 22, 77, 135, 165, 176, 199–200, 204, 212, 215.
Don't screen only "exceptional women". Keep household labour, inheritance, patronage and violence in view.

### Environment, food and water
1, 6, 9, 15, 17, 24, 31, 58, 112, 114, 173, 193, 198, 210, 214, 216.
Avoid environmental determinism. Pair climate or ecological evidence with institutions and with who was most vulnerable.

### Ideas, devotion and dissent
44–47, 83, 98, 157–165, 167–168, 200–201.
Keep texts and teachers tied to material support, translation, gender and rank.

### Economy, labour and exchange
19–22, 58, 72, 107, 112, 114, 120, 138, 173, 175, 191–193, 198, 208, 210, 214.
This path separates trade volume, state revenue, household production and coerced labour.

### Borderlands, migration and mobility
5, 26, 28, 38, 48, 50, 62–65, 82, 87, 94–95, 106, 119–121, 141, 154–155, 180, 198, 204, 206, 209.
"Borderland" describes a relationship to moving centres. It does not mean a peripheral people.

### Science, medicine and technology
2, 11, 15, 20, 31, 83, 98, 110, 197, 210, 215.
Credit craft knowledge, instruments, translation and institutions. Avoid contests over which civilization came first.

## Honest skip guidance

- Starting at S12 without S11 makes the Sultanate look like an unexplained invasion. Watch 121–122 and 132 first.
- Watching only S15 produces a Mughal-centred early modern history. Add 145–148, 155, 179 and 180.
- Jumping from the Mauryas to the Mughals erases a millennium. Use the sampler instead.
- A north-only route is not a survey of India. Add 34, 70, 89, 100, 109, 145 and 155.
- A "great religions" cut without 35, 96, 112, 161 and 165 loses labour, caste and gender.
- For 1857, watch 193–195. For Partition, watch 202–204. For the Constitution, watch 199, 201 and 205.
- Contemporary politics in 216 makes little sense without federalism (207), the Emergency (211), caste democracy (212), communal politics (213) and economic change (214).
- Never advertise an abbreviated path as comprehensive. State what it leaves out, in the syllabus and on the opening slate.
