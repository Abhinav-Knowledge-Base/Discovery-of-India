## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Jaunpur turns Delhi's crisis into a regional sultanate whose mosques, scholarship, and armies compete across the middle Ganges. |
| **Years** | 1394–1479 |
| **Before–after** | From Tughluq provincial center to Lodi conquest and remembered scholarly city. |
## Before
- Firoz Shah Tughluq develops Jaunpur as an eastern administrative center.
- Timur's invasion weakens Delhi but does not create an empty countryside.
## The turn
- Malik Sarwar and his successors establish Sharqi sovereignty.
- Jaunpur competes with Bengal, Odisha, and Delhi for routes and tribute.
## After
- Husain Shah Sharqi's campaigns against the Lodis fail.
- Lodi annexation ends the dynasty while urban institutions continue.
## Act I — Province becomes court
- Titles, coins, appointments, and khutba mark a gradual break from Delhi.
- The Sharqis inherit officials while recruiting local landed support.
- Territory remains negotiated beyond forts and river towns.
## Act II — Building Jaunpur
- Atala and Jama mosques use monumental pylons and local masonry.
- Temple spolia requires case-specific study, not a single universal explanation.
- Architecture creates a dynastic idiom often mislabeled as purely provincial.
## Act III — Knowledge and music
- Jaunpur's later “Shiraz of India” reputation reflects scholarly memory.
- Jurists, Sufis, poets, and musicians rely on patronage and institutions.
- Court narratives underrecord artisans, women, and nonelite students.
## Act IV — Ganges rivalries
- Sharqi armies challenge Bahlul Lodi through repeated, uncertain campaigns.
- Rajput chiefs and Afghan commanders pursue their own advantage.
- Religious identity does not fix alliance.
## Act V — Defeat and memory
- Bahlul absorbs Jaunpur after Husain Shah's reverses.
- Sikandar Lodi later attacks some Sharqi monuments and memories.
- The city's architectural language outlasts dynastic sovereignty.
## Subcontinental note
- Jaunpur shows that fifteenth-century North India had several political centers.
- Regional Islamicate culture was internally disputed and locally rooted.
## How we know
- Later chronicles often read Sharqi history through Lodi victory.
- Coins, inscriptions, masonry phases, and manuscripts anchor chronology.
- Musical attributions require caution when surviving copies are late.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Jaunpur was merely a Delhi imitation.
- Do not claim “Shiraz of India” is a neutral contemporary statistic.
- Do not claim all reused stone records one event.
- Do not claim Lodi conquest erased local elites.
