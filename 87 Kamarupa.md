# Ep 87 — Kamarupa

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | In the Brahmaputra valley, Kamarupa’s courts translate river, hill, forest, and frontier relationships into claims no outside empire fully contains. |
| **Years** | c. 350–750 CE |
| **Before–after** | Early polities of the Brahmaputra valley → Varman and Salastambha dynasties |

## Before
Valley farmers, hill communities, elephant forests, traders, ritual specialists, and routes toward Tibet, Bhutan, Bengal, and Myanmar formed a diverse region.

## The turn
Varman rulers issue grants and diplomacy; Bhaskaravarman’s alliance with Harsha gives Kamarupa unusual visibility in external accounts.

## After
Salastambha rulers succeed without ending local diversity or the valley’s shifting relations with hills and neighboring states.

## Act I — Naming a region
Separate textual Kamarupa from modern Assam and from later sacred geographies that retroactively enlarge it.

## Act II — Copper-plate sovereignty
Read Dubi and Nidhanpur charters as land and lineage claims, including the problem of who already used granted landscapes.

## Act III — Bhaskaravarman abroad
Use correspondence, assemblies, and Xuanzang to show diplomacy between peers rather than absorption into Harsha’s empire.

## Act IV — Valley and hills
Center river mobility, forest products, elephants, shifting cultivation, wet-rice zones, and communities absent from Sanskrit charters.

## Act V — After the Varmans
Treat dynastic change as one layer in a longer northeastern history, not a fall from connection into isolation.

## Subcontinental note
The Northeast enters through its own centers and links to Bengal, Nepal, Tibet, Bhutan, and Southeast Asia; it is not a frontier blank attached to “India proper.”

## How we know
Copper plates, seals, archaeology, place names, Xuanzang, and later chronicles illuminate court networks but poorly record many hill and forest societies.

## Do not claim
- Do not equate Kamarupa’s borders with modern Assam or the entire Northeast.
- Do not call Bhaskaravarman a subordinate of Harsha.
- Do not treat chartered land as empty before a grant.
- Do not assign modern ethnic identities mechanically to ancient names.
- Do not use sparse monuments as evidence of regional backwardness.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |

| Pushyavarman | Genealogical founder of the Varman line |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Brahmaputra channels, Guwahati, Da Parbatia, plate facsimiles, elephant corridors, fields, and hill routes; avoid hard boundary polygons.
