# Ep 96 — Temples, Land Grants, and New Social Orders

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Copper plates and temple walls record new claims over land and labor, but their varied outcomes resist a single “Indian feudalism” formula. |
| **Years** | c. 400–1000 CE |
| **Before–after** | Expanding regional courts → denser networks of endowed settlements, temples, monasteries, and intermediaries |

## Before
Gifts to Brahmins and religious institutions had earlier precedents, while cultivators, pastoralists, artisans, and forest communities held layered local rights.

## The turn
Royal and non-royal grants multiply, temples accumulate resources, and status groups negotiate new agrarian, ritual, and political relationships.

## After
Regional social orders become more inscriptionally visible, though neither caste nor landed power follows one subcontinental timeline.

## Act I — A plate is a claim
Open a copper charter’s seal, genealogy, boundaries, taxes, exemptions, witnesses, and curse while asking whether its terms were enforced.

## Act II — Making fields
Trace irrigation, clearing, migration, soil knowledge, and forest products without describing uncultivated land as empty.

## Act III — Temple economies
Show temples as ritual, redistributive, credit, craft, performance, and political institutions whose scale differed sharply.

## Act IV — Ordering people
Compare varna language, jati formation, untouchability evidence, women donors, inheritance, bonded labor, and occupational mobility without false uniformity.

## Act V — The feudalism debate
Present models of decentralization, samanta politics, trade contraction, commercialization, and regionalization alongside their critics.

## Subcontinental note
Patterns differed across Kashmir, Bengal, the Deccan, Tamilakam, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and forest frontiers; comparison must not annex distinct histories.

## How we know
Charters and temple inscriptions are normative transactions; archaeology, pollen, settlement surveys, coins, and literature test but do not complete their picture.

## Do not claim
- Do not say land grants automatically created feudalism everywhere.
- Do not assume granted land was vacant or unused.
- Do not read charter exemptions as proof they worked uniformly.
- Do not treat caste categories as static across regions.
- Do not claim temples replaced all trade, states, or local institutions.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Damodarpur plates | Gupta-era archive of land transactions |
| Nidhanpur plates | Grant and regrant in Kamarupa-linked territory |
| Velvikudi plates | Pandya memory of land loss and restoration |
| Uttaramerur inscriptions | Detailed but local evidence for assembly procedures |
| Nalanda endowments | Monastic land and transregional patronage |
| Samanta | Flexible political term central to scholarly debate |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film plate manufacture, boundary landscapes, tanks, forest edges, temple storerooms, inscriptions, and excavated villages; visualize competing scholarly models.
