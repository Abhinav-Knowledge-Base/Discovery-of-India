# Ep 82 — Sri Lanka and the Southern Sea

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Anuradhapura’s rulers, monasteries, reservoirs, and ports connect Sri Lanka to South Asia while sustaining an island history not reducible to the mainland. |
| **Years** | c. 250–600 CE |
| **Before–after** | Mature Anuradhapura polity → intensified South Indian and Buddhist maritime connections |

## Before
The island held diverse settlements and languages, powerful monasteries, irrigation systems, ports, and political contests beyond later ethnic binaries.

## The turn
Royal and monastic institutions organize water, land, relics, and texts while diplomacy and conflict cross the Palk Strait and Bay of Bengal.

## After
Anuradhapura remains influential, but succession struggles and regional exchanges foreshadow later Pallava, Pandya, and Chola entanglements.

## Act I — An island center
Map Sri Lanka from Anuradhapura outward, avoiding a camera route that treats it only as an appendix to India.

## Act II — Reservoir and field
Examine tanks, channels, monastic estates, cultivators, and labor while rejecting hydraulic determinism.

## Act III — Buddhist plurality
Mahavihara, Abhayagiri, and Jetavana competed and exchanged ideas; “Theravada” was not a timeless institutional uniformity.

## Act IV — Across the strait
Trace merchants, monks, envoys, mercenaries, brides, and armies moving between distinct polities.

## Act V — Chronicles and memory
Read the *Mahavamsa* as powerful monastic historiography, attentive to its silences about women, non-elites, and rival communities.

## Subcontinental note
Sri Lanka belongs in connected history through agency and reciprocity, not by being rhetorically annexed to an Indian civilizational map.

## How we know
Chronicles, Brahmi inscriptions, archaeology, coins, reservoirs, sculpture, and external records use different calendars and political vocabularies.

## Do not claim
- Do not call Sri Lanka culturally or politically part of an Indian empire without evidence.
- Do not project modern Sinhala–Tamil identities unchanged into this period.
- Do not treat the *Mahavamsa* as neutral chronology.
- Do not assume reservoirs prove a fully centralized state.
- Do not erase Hindu, Jain, local, and varied Buddhist practices.
