# Ep 104 — Kashmir After the Karkotas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Queens, ministers, landlords, soldiers, poets, and engineers contest Kashmir after Karkota rule, exposing politics beyond a sequence of kings. |
| **Years** | c. 855–1003 CE |
| **Before–after** | Karkota collapse → Utpala, Yashaskara, and Lohara transitions |

## Before
Karkota monuments and trans-Himalayan ties endured as court factions, landed interests, religious institutions, and military groups competed.

## The turn
Avantivarman stabilizes Utpala rule and sponsors works associated with Suyya; later succession struggles give powerful queens unusual visibility.

## After
Didda consolidates power and prepares the Lohara succession, while Kashmir remains linked to mountain and plains networks.

## Act I — Reading Kalhana carefully
Use the *Rajatarangini*’s detail while testing its moral tales against coins, monuments, and chronology.

## Act II — River engineering
Present Suyya’s flood and irrigation works as technically plausible but literarily embellished and dependent on extensive labor.

## Act III — Temples and towns
Avantipur monuments reveal resources, artisans, and political theology but not the living standards of the entire valley.

## Act IV — Queens in power
Follow Sugandha and Didda without turning exceptional sovereignty into proof of general gender equality or repeating misogynistic tropes.

## Act V — Damaras and countryside
Examine landed military groups, cultivators, pastoralists, taxes, and local conflict through a court source that labels its opponents.

## Subcontinental note
Kashmir’s ties to Punjab, Kabul routes, Tibet, and Central Asia made it a connected center without subordinating those neighboring histories.

## How we know
Kalhana’s twelfth-century chronicle, coins, temple archaeology, sculpture, and limited inscriptions provide rich stories but few contemporary checks.

## Do not claim
- Do not treat Kalhana’s speeches and anecdotes as verbatim records.
- Do not attribute all hydrological change to Suyya alone.
- Do not infer broad gender equality from ruling queens.
- Do not accept court labels for Damaras as neutral sociology.
- Do not depict Kashmir as isolated or permanently united.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Avantivarman | Founder of Utpala rule |
| Suyya | Engineer-minister celebrated for river works |
| Sugandha | Queen who exercised sovereign authority |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Jhelum channels, Avantipur, coins of queens, irrigation landscapes, passes, and manuscripts; distinguish Kalhana’s scene-setting from material evidence.
