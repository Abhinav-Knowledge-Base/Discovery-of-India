# Ep 79 — The Pallavas Begin

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Elusive charters and changing languages show Pallava power taking shape between Andhra corridors and northern Tamilakam. |
| **Years** | c. 275–575 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-Satavahana and post-Sangam polities → a Pallava center at Kanchipuram |

## Before
Southern Andhra and northern Tamil regions held local chiefs, traders, cultivators, pastoralists, forest communities, Buddhist, Jain, and Brahmanical institutions.

## The turn
Early Pallava rulers issue Prakrit and then Sanskrit charters, claim lineages, grant revenues, and compete for routes and settlements.

## After
By Simhavishnu’s era, a more visible Kanchipuram court sets the stage for conflict and monumental patronage.

## Act I — An origin problem
Lay out competing hypotheses about Pallava origins and explain why linguistic resemblance or later genealogy cannot settle them.

## Act II — Charters in Prakrit
Read Mayidavolu and Hirehadagalli as acts of authority whose formulas reveal ambition more readily than exact borders.

## Act III — Toward Kanchi
Track the court’s southern orientation while distinguishing archaeological evidence from later literary memory.

## Act IV — Settlement and hierarchy
Treat brahmadeya-style gifts as negotiated interventions in agrarian and social landscapes, with effects varying by place.

## Act V — A dynasty becomes legible
End with Simhavishnu, but keep Kalabhras, Pandyas, Cholas, Gangas, Kadambas, and local powers in frame.

## Subcontinental note
Pallava beginnings belong to Bay of Bengal and Deccan networks as well as Tamil history; Sri Lankan contacts were exchanges and conflicts between separate polities.

## How we know
Copper-plate charters, seals, coins, pottery, archaeology, and much later literary traditions leave major gaps in chronology and succession.

## Do not claim
- Do not present any single Pallava-origin theory as established.
- Do not project the later Kanchipuram empire unchanged into early charters.
- Do not equate a grant’s claim with complete control on the ground.
- Do not turn the Kalabhra interval into a source-free “dark age.”
- Do not infer uniform caste or gender practice from prescriptive formulas.

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Kanchipuram layers, charter plates and seals, Andhra–Tamil route landscapes, ceramics, and tanks; display rival chronologies side by side.
