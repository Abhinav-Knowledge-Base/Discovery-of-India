# Ep 97 — The Pala Realm

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Elected in a crisis according to later praise, Gopala’s dynasty binds Bengal and Bihar through armies, grants, monasteries, and shifting alliances. |
| **Years** | c. 750–1160 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-Shashanka contest → Pala ascendancy, revivals, and Sena displacement |

## Before
Gauda, Vanga, Samatata, Varendra, Magadha, forest tracts, ports, and delta communities followed distinct but connected trajectories.

## The turn
Gopala, Dharmapala, and Devapala construct paramountcy, contest Kannauj, and patronize Buddhist and Brahmanical institutions.

## After
Pala power contracts and revives more than once before Sena and regional rulers dominate much of its former realm.

## Act I — Chosen king?
Interrogate the Khalimpur claim that people elected Gopala to end *matsyanyaya*, a legitimating story rather than a modern ballot.

## Act II — The Kannauj contest
Cross-cut Pala, Pratihara, and Rashtrakuta claims to expose overlapping victories and temporary alliances.

## Act III — Rivers and regions
Use delta channels, Varendra, Magadha, elephants, boats, and agrarian expansion to complicate a capital-centered map.

## Act IV — Buddhist kings, plural patronage
Set great monasteries beside Shaiva, Vaishnava, local, and Brahmanical grants; royal affiliation did not standardize subjects’ religion.

## Act V — Labor beneath bronze
Follow metalworkers, manuscript painters, cultivators, fishers, women donors, and forest communities faintly visible in elite archives.

## Subcontinental note
Pala diplomacy reached Nepal, Tibet, Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia through independent partners and religious networks, not imperial annexation.

## How we know
Copper plates, monastery seals, sculpture, manuscripts, archaeology, Tibetan histories, and rival prashastis provide changing and partisan maps.

## Do not claim
- Do not describe Gopala’s “election” as modern democracy.
- Do not paint all Bengal and Bihar as continuously Pala-controlled.
- Do not call the realm exclusively Buddhist.
- Do not treat monastery patronage as evidence of universal prosperity.
- Do not make agrarian growth synonymous with empty-forest settlement.

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Somapura, Varendra landscapes, Bihar monastery sites, bronzes, manuscripts, and river travel; distinguish inscriptional reach from direct rule.
