# Ep 88 — Kashmir and the Karkotas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Karkota rulers turn Kashmir’s valley and mountain corridors into a formidable center, reconstructed through archaeology and a much later chronicle. |
| **Years** | c. 625–855 CE |
| **Before–after** | Post-Huna Kashmir → Karkota expansion and Utpala succession |

## Before
The valley sustained Buddhist and Brahmanical institutions, irrigation, crafts, and routes toward Gandhara, the Punjab, Ladakh, and Central Asia.

## The turn
Durlabhavardhana’s dynasty consolidates, while Lalitaditya’s later memory projects campaigns and monumental patronage far beyond the valley.

## After
Court struggle ends Karkota rule, but temple landscapes, learning, and trans-Himalayan connections continue.

## Act I — Valley and passes
Begin with ecology, winter, water, uplands, and movement to resist portraying mountains as sealed borders.

## Act II — Chronicle after empire
Introduce Kalhana’s twelfth-century *Rajatarangini* as critical historiography composed centuries after Karkota events.

## Act III — Lalitaditya’s horizons
Compare the chronicle’s conquests with Tang records, archaeology, and limited inscriptions; preserve uncertainty over scale and route.

## Act IV — Martand and Parihasapura
Treat monuments as organized labor, material supply, ritual theater, and political claim, not proof of effortless prosperity.

## Act V — Plural Kashmir
Follow Buddhist, Shaiva, Vaishnava, and local traditions alongside gendered court stories, landholding, artisans, and pastoral communities.

## Subcontinental note
Kashmir’s connections to Tibet, Central Asia, Gandhara, and northern plains do not make it a peripheral possession of any one civilizational core.

## How we know
Kalhana, Chinese records, coins, sculpture, temple archaeology, and sparse inscriptions differ sharply in date; campaign maps are especially conjectural.

## Do not claim
- Do not map Lalitaditya’s literary conquests as a securely administered empire.
- Do not treat the *Rajatarangini* as contemporary reportage.
- Do not describe Kashmir as isolated by its mountains.
- Do not infer universal wealth from royal temples.
- Do not collapse Buddhist and Brahmanical plurality into modern communal categories.

## Figures, sites, and works

## Locations and visual evidence
Film valley waterways, passes, Martand, Parihasapura, sculpture, coins, and manuscript folios; style uncertain campaigns as routes, not filled territories.
