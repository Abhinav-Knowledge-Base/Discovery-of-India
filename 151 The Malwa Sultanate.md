## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | From Mandu's plateau, Malwa's sultans contest central India while courts, farms, and waterworks sustain a vulnerable kingdom. |
| **Years** | 1401–1562 |
| **Before–after** | From Delhi's provincial fracture to Mughal incorporation. |
## Before
- Malwa commands routes among Gujarat, Rajasthan, the Deccan, and Gangetic India.
- Paramara memories and Delhi institutions remain in the landscape.
## The turn
- Dilawar Khan and Hoshang Shah convert governorship into sovereignty.
- Mandu's defensible plateau becomes a capital of palaces, mosques, and reservoirs.
## After
- Khalji rulers expand amid wars with Gujarat, Mewar, and neighbors.
- Baz Bahadur loses Malwa to Akbar's forces in 1561–62.
## Act I — Plateau capital
- Read gates, ravines, tanks, and roads as military infrastructure.
- Monumental Mandu depends on villages and coerced agrarian surplus below.
- Hoshang Shah's tomb projects dynastic legitimacy.
## Act II — Khalji ambition
- Mahmud Khalji campaigns against Rana Kumbha with mixed outcomes.
- Victory monuments on both sides turn inconclusive war into triumph.
- Forts and tributaries make sovereignty uneven.
## Act III — Courtly worlds
- The *Nimatnama* depicts recipes, hunting, medicine, and elite pleasure.
- Its painting combines several workshops and visual conventions.
- Courtly refinement does not represent ordinary diets or gender relations.
## Act IV — Between powers
- Gujarat repeatedly intervenes in Malwa succession and territory.
- Rajput, Afghan, and Muslim commanders cross dynastic service.
- Sectarian labels cannot substitute for political analysis.
## Act V — Baz Bahadur after legend
- Separate evidence for the ruler from later romance with Rupmati.
- Mughal commander Adham Khan's invasion includes violence and plunder.
- Regional resistance continues after formal annexation.
## Subcontinental note
- Malwa's “centrality” comes from routes and rival claims, not geographic destiny.
- Courtly exchange coexists with coercion, warfare, and unstable patronage.
## How we know
- Persian chronicles frame campaigns around royal honor.
- Inscriptions and monuments preserve rival victory claims.
- The *Nimatnama*, coins, reservoirs, and archaeology broaden court history.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Mandu's palaces were isolated pleasure retreats.
- Do not claim Rupmati legends are contemporary biography.
- Do not claim style alone identifies artisans' religion.
- Do not claim Mughal annexation immediately pacified Malwa.
