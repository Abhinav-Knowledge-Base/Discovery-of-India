## Before

Brahmanical learning restrictions, caste labor, child marriage, widowhood, and patriarchal authority limited education and public voice.

## The turn

The Phules opened schools, organized Satyashodhak networks, sheltered vulnerable women, and named caste domination as structural power.

## After

Their writing and practice shaped non-Brahman, Dalit, feminist, peasant, and educational movements while generating continuing debate.

## Act I - Girls' education

Savitribai and Fatima Sheikh taught despite harassment, making pedagogy a struggle over social authority.

## Act II - Caste as history and power

Jyotirao's Gulamgiri attacked sacred legitimation and connected exploitation to material relations.

## Act III - Care as politics

The couple supported widows, opposed infanticide, and challenged control over sexuality and reproduction.

## Act IV - Satyashodhak Samaj

Ritual alternatives and organization sought dignity beyond Brahman mediation.

## Act V - Cultivator's question

Shetkaryacha Asud criticized revenue, bureaucracy, landlords, and elite indifference.

## Subcontinental note

The movement centered Maharashtra but conversed with missionary education, global abolition, princely reform, and later anti-caste politics across India.

## How we know

Marathi books, poems, school reports, hostile newspapers, biographies, institutional records, and movement memory require attention to attribution and context.

## Do not claim

- Jyotirao alone created the movement.
- Girls' schooling was benevolent reform from above.
- Anti-caste politics rejected all religion in one way.
- The Phules' program solved caste inequality.
