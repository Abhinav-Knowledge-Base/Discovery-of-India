## Before

Regional associations, vernacular publics, caste organizations, and economic critics preceded the Indian National Congress.

## The turn

Swadeshi, revolutionary networks, trade unions, kisan sabhas, and Congress factions broadened politics beyond annual elite meetings.

## After

By the Second World War, mass constituencies shared opposition to empire but disagreed over class, caste, communal representation, and tactics.

## Act I - Congress and critique

Moderates built petitions and economic arguments; assertive nationalists promoted boycott and mass symbols.

## Act II - Revolutionary worlds

Networks in Bengal, Punjab, Maharashtra, abroad, and prison used violence, sacrifice, print, and transnational aid.

## Act III - Workers organize

Bombay textile, railway, plantation, dock, and other workers formed unions amid repression and political rivalry.

## Act IV - Peasant movements

Champaran, Bardoli, Awadh, Telangana precursors, and kisan sabhas joined local grievances to wider platforms.

## Act V - Competing futures

Socialists, communists, liberals, caste movements, and communal organizations disputed the nation to be built.

## Subcontinental note

Politics crossed India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh's later borders and reached London, North America, Southeast Asia, and the Soviet world.

## How we know

Congress proceedings, vernacular papers, intelligence files, union minutes, workers' songs, trial records, oral histories, and memoirs carry strategic silences.

## Do not claim

- Congress alone constituted nationalism.
- All revolutionaries shared one ideology.
- Workers and peasants merely followed urban leaders.
- Anti-colonial unity erased caste, class, or communal conflict.
