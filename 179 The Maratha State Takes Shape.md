## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Shivaji and his successors turn Deccan service, hill forts, coastal power, and revenue claims into a resilient Maratha state. |
| **Years** | c. 1630–1707 |
| **Before–after** | From Bhonsle service under Deccan sultanates to a distributed kingdom surviving Mughal invasion. |
## Before
- Maratha lineages serve Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda, and Mughal employers.
- Deshmukhs, cultivators, forts, and devotional institutions structure western Deccan power.
## The turn
- Shivaji captures forts and revenue zones while negotiating and fighting Bijapur.
- His 1674 coronation asserts Kshatriya sovereignty through newly assembled ritual legitimacy.
## After
- Sambhaji's execution does not end resistance.
- Rajaram, Tarabai, commanders, and local networks disperse and sustain war.
## Act I — A Deccan formation
- Shahji's mobile career provides military resources and political models.
- Jijabai's household role matters without turning legend into sole causation.
- “Maratha” identity includes varied castes, ranks, and regional interests.
## Act II — Fort and countryside
- Torna, Rajgad, and other forts anchor intelligence, storage, and local collection.
- Light cavalry and negotiated village support enable rapid movement.
- Raiding can be strategy and coercive burden simultaneously.
## Act III — Bijapur and Afzal Khan
- The 1659 meeting ends with Afzal Khan killed amid mutual suspicion.
- Later retellings moralize an encounter both sides prepared militarily.
- Victory brings arms, followers, and symbolic momentum.
## Act IV — Mughals and coronation
- Shaista Khan's defeat and the Surat raid challenge imperial prestige.
- The Agra escape follows submission negotiations and confinement.
- Coronation patronage resolves contested rank while taxing the realm.
## Act V — Distributed survival
- Sambhaji governs amid Mughal invasion, Portuguese rivalry, and internal disputes.
- His brutal execution intensifies but does not create Maratha resistance.
- Tarabai's leadership shows dynastic and regional agency after Rajaram.
## Subcontinental note
- The Maratha state grows from Deccan institutions, not an isolated national awakening.
- Religious idioms matter alongside revenue, caste status, fort strategy, and sovereignty.
## How we know
- Marathi bakhars are often late and partisan.
- Persian chronicles, European factory records, letters, grants, forts, and account terms provide checks.
- Coronation texts describe claims, not uncontested social acceptance.
## Do not claim
- Do not call Shivaji a modern nationalist.
- Do not depict Marathas or Muslims as unified opposing blocs.
- Do not romanticize raids or erase civilian costs.
- Do not reduce Jijabai or Tarabai to inspirational symbols.
