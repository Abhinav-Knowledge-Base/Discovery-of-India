## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | Kannada-speaking devotees challenge ritual and caste authority, then build durable Lingayat institutions with their own hierarchies. |
| **Years** | c. 1100–1300, with later codification |
| **Before–after** | From Kalyana's courtly ferment to organized Virashaiva/Lingayat communities. |
## Before
- Western Chalukya and Kalachuri courts support temples, Brahmans, Jains, and artisans.
- Personal linga traditions predate the best-known twelfth-century movement.
## The turn
- Basava's circle uses vachanas to debate labor, worship, caste, and embodiment.
- Kalyana conflict becomes a foundational memory of persecution and dispersal.
## After
- Monasteries, gurus, ritual specialists, and literary canons stabilize communities.
- Later Virashaiva theology reorganizes diverse early voices.
## Act I — Kalyana's social field
- Basava serves in Bijjala's court, linking critique to state employment.
- Artisans, cultivators, women, and elites appear among named sharanas.
- The Anubhava Mantapa is institutionally clearer in later memory than contemporary evidence.
## Act II — Vachana speech
- Short Kannada compositions reject empty ritual and claim direct devotion to Shiva.
- Performance and manuscript collection separate composition from surviving text.
- Radical language does not prove uniform social practice.
## Act III — Work and caste
- Kayaka dignifies work; dasoha frames sharing and service.
- Inter-caste marriage traditions expose the limits of court tolerance.
- Caste critique coexists with later endogamy and graded institutions.
## Act IV — Women and bodies
- Akka Mahadevi's poems confront marriage, clothing, desire, and authority.
- Other women vachana writers reveal household and occupational worlds.
- Hagiography can domesticate voices it celebrates.
## Act V — Institution after rupture
- Traditions narrate violence at Kalyana and community dispersal.
- Mathas preserve teaching, property, education, and political influence.
- Distinguish modern Lingayat identity debates from medieval categories.
## Subcontinental note
- The movement belongs to Kannada religious, occupational, and courtly history.
- It cannot be reduced to either timeless Hindu reform or wholly separate modern identity.
## How we know
- Vachanas survive in later manuscripts with attribution problems.
- Inscriptions identify patrons and institutions but less often dissenters.
- Hagiographies, temple forms, portable lingas, and oral practice require source criticism.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Basava single-handedly founded every Lingayat practice.
- Do not claim the movement permanently ended caste.
- Do not claim Anubhava Mantapa operated like a modern parliament.
- Do not project current legal identities unchanged into the twelfth century.
