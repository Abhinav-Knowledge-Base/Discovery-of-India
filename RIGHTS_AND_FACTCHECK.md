# Rights and fact-check protocol

## Staged workflow

| Gate | Timing | Required output | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Scope | Season architecture | Question, geographic rule, living-risk screen | Showrunner + research lead |
| 1. Research | Before outline | Annotated bibliography, evidence matrix, source gaps | Lead historian |
| 2. Claims | Outline | Numbered claim sheet: wording, class, citation, confidence, dispute | Fact-check editor |
| 3. Script | Each draft | Line check; quotation/translation/pronunciation log; counterevidence | Specialist + language editor |
| 4. Visual | Before shoot/archive order | Map ledger, artifact/site IDs, reconstruction dossier | Archaeology/art/map leads |
| 5. Rights | Before rough cut | Rights IDs, quote/clip/still/music/art status and restrictions | Rights producer |
| 6. Sensitive review | Before picture lock | Harm, privacy, communal, legal and security assessment | Standards + counsel |
| 7. Lock | Picture lock | Final claim audit against actual edit and captions | Fact-check editor + counsel |
| 8. Release | Master delivery | Credits, licences, cue sheets, accessibility and correction route | Executive producer |
| 9. Post-release | Continuing | Correction log, versioned masters and source updates | Managing editor |

No “fair use/fair dealing” assumption enters a cut without jurisdiction-specific counsel. Publicly accessible does not mean public domain.

## Claim labels and source tiers

| Tier | Material | Proper use |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Securely provenanced primary evidence; critical edition; official record with known context; peer-reviewed excavation/dataset | Anchor factual claims within its limits |
| 2 | Peer-reviewed scholarship or major scholarly monograph synthesizing Tier 1 | Interpretation and context; check age and debate |
| 3 | Specialist museum catalogue, academic reference work, reputable edited volume | Orientation and supported detail; trace onward |
| 4 | Credible journalism, institutional explainer, documentary interview | Contemporary context and leads, not sole support for disputed historical claims |
| 5 | Memoir, oral history, community tradition, literary memory | Essential evidence of experience/memory; do not force into event proof |
| 6 | Tourism copy, unsourced website, social post, generative output | Leads only; never final authority |

Every factual line requires a Tier 1–3 source unless it is explicitly framed as testimony/memory. High-risk claims require two independent evidentiary routes where possible.

## Contested-claim register

| Topic | Unsafe shortcut | Required framing/evidence | Mandatory review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ancient migration | “Invasion” or “indigenous” as settled identity slogans | Separate language models, archaeology, genetics and chronology; distinguish movement from conquest and modern identity | Archaeogeneticist, archaeologist, historical linguist |
| Indus language/religion | Declaring the script deciphered; naming gods or rulers | State script remains undeciphered; compare sign patterns and material contexts; label iconographic inference | Indus specialist, epigrapher |
| Vedic–Harappan relation | Equating text and assemblage one-to-one | Show chronological/geographic overlap and limits; no ethnic label from pottery | Vedic philologist, archaeologist |
| Caste | Timeless four-varna system or wholly colonial invention | Distinguish varna/jati, prescription/practice, region, occupation, state enumeration, resistance and change | Social historian, Dalit studies scholar, regional specialist |
| Temple destruction | Civilizational/religious essence or denial of documented acts | Date actor, target, political context, source genre, rebuilding/reuse and comparable political violence | Medieval historians, epigrapher, legal/standards |
| Conversion | Force-versus-choice binary | Attend patronage, conviction, marriage, mobility, coercion, institutions, generations and source silence | Religion/social historians, regional language scholar |
| Hindu–Muslim relations | Two fixed communities across a millennium | Use period-specific institutions, sects, castes, languages, alliances and conflicts | Multi-specialist review |
| 1857 | Only “mutiny” or uniformly national “first war” | Name distinct military, courtly, peasant, urban and regional projects; explain labels and exclusions | Colonial/military/social historians |
| Colonial famine | Weather-only or one exact death number | Climate, prices, entitlement, policy, grain movement and ranges; identify method | Economic/environmental historian, demographer |
| Partition | Inevitable ancient hatred or leaders-only decision | Long and short causes, provincial variation, gender/caste/class, Punjab/Bengal/Sindh/Jammu and Kashmir, oral memory | India/Pakistan/Bangladesh scholars, trauma adviser |
| Kashmir | One uncontested legal or national story | Chronology of princely rule, accession, war, UN processes, constitutional change, regional voices and disputed claims | Multiple-region historians, international-law counsel, standards |
| Pakistan/Bangladesh | Appendices to Indian history | Retain autonomous political and social trajectories where shared history diverges | Pakistan/Bangladesh consultants |
| Adivasi histories | Isolated “tribes” outside history | Name communities and self-identifications; show trade, states, land, forest law and resistance | Community advisers, Adivasi studies scholar |
| Gender/sexuality | Great women plus victimhood | Household labour, property, embodiment, law, institutions, agency and source silence | Gender historian, safeguarding adviser |
| Genetics | Genes equal caste/language/religion/nation | Explain sample, date, statistical model, uncertainty and ethical governance | Population geneticist + archaeologist |
| Contemporary politics | Present party claim narrated as settled history | Date cut-off, primary documentation, proportional response, law and data; separate fact and analysis | Contemporary historians, data editor, counsel |

The register stores claim wording, claimant, source, counterevidence, affected episodes, risk, reviewers, disposition and final timecode. “Contested” does not mean unknowable or evenly balanced.

## Legal and ethical rights ledger

Each asset ID records: description; creator; depicted work/person; date; source/custodian; copyright owner; underlying rights; performer/privacy rights; moral rights; cultural/community protocol; media; territory; language; term; exclusivity; promotional use; edit/colourization restrictions; credit; fee; contract; expiry; delivery spec; status.

### Archive footage and photographs

Verify original negative/master and caption. Separate physical custody from copyright. Clear embedded music, art, performance and trademarks when needed. Do not license an agency copy before checking whether the underlying owner must also consent. Record editorial context restrictions; reject terms granting a source factual approval.

### Manuscripts, inscriptions, monuments and art

Copyright status of an ancient work does not settle photography, reproduction, database, site, museum or contractual rights. Obtain location permission and image licence separately. Credit repository, shelf mark/accession, folio/object, photographer and date. Sacred or funerary material may require community restrictions beyond law.

### Music

For every cue clear composition/publishing and master/recording rights, plus performer agreements. “Traditional” requires research into arrangement, collector, performers and community protocols. Commission contracts specify stems, soundtrack/album, promo, term, territory, platforms and reuse. No temp track survives lock.

### Interviews and oral histories

Use plain-language informed consent describing topic, platforms, territory, term, clips/promotion, archive deposit, withdrawal deadline and risk. Consent is a process, not merely a release. Separate factual transcript review from editorial approval. Minors and vulnerable contributors require enhanced safeguarding. Existing oral histories retain original consent limits.

### Locations and reconstructions

Secure owner/custodian permission, permits, drone approval, insurance and conservation conditions. Reconstructed assets require designer/artist contracts and disclosure. No protected-site access is implied by public opening.

## Correction log

| ID | Episode/timecode | Published claim | Issue/source | Classification | Decision/correction | Version/date | Audience notice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — | — | — | — | Factual / translation / caption / rights / interpretation | Open | — | — |

Rules:
1. Acknowledge reports within five working days.
2. Preserve the released wording and evidence; never silently erase the audit trail.
3. Correct factual, translation, caption and rights errors promptly in all controllable versions.
4. Interpretive updates receive a dated note when scholarship materially changes.
5. High-harm errors trigger distribution pause and counsel review.
6. Notify partners holding superseded masters.
7. Publish material corrections in accessible language; do not hide them only in end credits.

## Final lock questions

- Can every map, quotation, number, image, sound and translated term be traced?
- Does the final edit preserve the qualifiers present in the research?
- Are serious counterarguments represented at their actual evidentiary weight?
- Has anyone been placed at avoidable legal, communal, physical or reputational risk?
- Are all actual clearances documented—not merely requested, assumed or marked “public”?
- Does the episode distinguish evidence, inference and memory in voice, image and caption?
