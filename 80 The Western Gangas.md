# Ep 80 — The Western Gangas

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | A long-lived southern Karnataka dynasty survives stronger neighbors through local alliances, landholding networks, and plural religious patronage. |
| **Years** | c. 350–1000 CE |
| **Before–after** | Early Karnataka chiefdoms → Ganga rule under Chalukya, Rashtrakuta, and Chola pressure |

## Before
Southern Karnataka’s uplands supported farming, herding, forests, exchange routes, Jain communities, Brahmanical institutions, and many local authorities.

## The turn
Ganga rulers establish centers at Kolar and Talakad and bind military households, landed elites, temples, monasteries, and villages through grants.

## After
Chola conquest ends independent Ganga rule, while elites, inscriptions, Jain monuments, and regional institutions continue.

## Act I — A difficult beginning
Compare later origin legends with early inscriptions, refusing certainty where genealogy and chronology conflict.

## Act II — Talakad’s river world
Use the Kaveri landscape to connect irrigation, agrarian expansion, pastoral zones, and forest access.

## Act III — Between larger powers
Show Gangas as strategic actors in Pallava, Chalukya, and Rashtrakuta politics rather than passive feudatories.

## Act IV — Religion and social power
Jain patronage, Brahmanical gifts, hero stones, and women’s records reveal plurality and hierarchy without fully recovering ordinary lives.

## Act V — Shravana Belgola
Build toward Chamundaraya’s Gommateshwara commission, distinguishing ministerial patronage from a dynasty-wide religious identity.

## Subcontinental note
Ganga Karnataka connected the Tamil plains, Deccan plateau, western coast, and Sri Lankan-linked commerce without becoming a mere corridor between “greater” states.

## How we know
Kannada and Sanskrit inscriptions, copper plates, hero stones, monuments, and literary works are abundant late but sparse for the dynasty’s beginnings.

## Do not claim
- Do not treat legendary migration stories as established origins.
- Do not label the Gangas permanently independent or permanently subordinate.
- Do not infer universal Jain dominance from elite monuments.
- Do not reduce land grants to an automatic feudal stage.
- Do not read hero stones as complete evidence for gender or warrior society.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|

| Durvinita | Ruler associated with warfare and literary learning |

## Locations and visual evidence
Film Talakad, Kolar region, Shravana Belgola, hero stones, irrigation works, and forest margins; subtitle inscription language and date visibly.
