## Before

The Deccan contained older sultanate institutions, landed elites, multilingual towns, and military households.

## The turn

The Asaf Jahs became autonomous in practice while preserving Mughal forms and navigating Maratha and European competition.

## After

Subsidiary alliance narrowed sovereignty, yet the state retained institutions until accession and armed integration in 1948.

## Act I - An Asaf Jahi state

The Nizams balanced nobles, contractors, zamindars, and military pressures.

## Act II - Alliance and constraint

Subsidiary troops tied finances to Company strategy.

## Act III - Court and city

Urdu, Telugu, Marathi, Persian, and Dakhni worlds met amid hierarchy.

## Act IV - Reform and inequality

University and bureaucracy coexisted with jagirs and caste oppression.

## Act V - Late princely crisis

Popular movements, Razakars, Telangana struggle, and 1948 require distinct evidence.

## Subcontinental note

The state encompassed Telangana, Marathwada, and Kannada-speaking districts; one linguistic or communal story cannot represent all subjects.

## How we know

Daftar records, treaties, newspapers, oral histories, architecture, police files, and the Sundarlal report illuminate different scales.

## Do not claim

- Hyderabad was homogeneous.
- Subsidiary alliance ended all internal agency.
- 1948 was a bloodless integration.
- The court represented every subject equally.
