## Treatment
| Field | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | On the Tungabhadra frontier, Sangama chiefs build a kingdom through war, temple patronage, and negotiated local power. |
| **Years** | c. 1320s–1377 |
| **Before–after** | From Kampili, Hoysala, Kakatiya, and Delhi disruptions to an expanding Sangama state. |
## Before
- Southern polities already possessed durable agrarian, military, and temple networks.
- Delhi's Deccan campaigns altered sovereignty without erasing local elites.
## The turn
- Harihara and Bukka appear in records before later legends stabilize their origins.
- Control of river crossings and forts makes the Tungabhadra basin strategic.
## After
- Sangama rulers compete with Madurai, Bahmani, and surviving regional powers.
- A retrospective foundation story turns contested beginnings into dynastic destiny.
## Act I — A fractured Deccan
- Map Kampili, Hoysala, Kakatiya, Pandya, and Delhi claims as overlapping fields.
- Show refugees, commanders, merchants, and cultivators moving through political rupture.
- Treat “Hindu response” as a later simplification, not an adequate founding cause.
## Act II — Brothers in the record
- Introduce Harihara I and Bukka I through dated inscriptions and grants.
- Compare epigraphic evidence with Vidyaranya conversion and reconversion traditions.
- Ask why courts invent clean beginnings for rulers with mobile affiliations.
## Act III — River, fort, and field
- Follow Anegundi and the Tungabhadra's defensible granite landscape.
- Explain revenue, irrigation, horse supply, and military retainers behind conquest.
- Temple endowments connect kingship to Brahman, artisanal, and landed institutions.
## Act IV — Expanding claims
- Track movement toward Hoysala territories and Tamil country without implying instant control.
- Local nayakas bargain, resist, and transmit resources to the new court.
- Raichur becomes a recurring zone of conflict, not a fixed civilizational border.
## Act V — Remembering a foundation
- Set later chronicles beside sixteenth-century monumental landscapes.
- Show how “City of Victory” projected permanence backward onto improvisation.
- End with Bukka's succession and a state still being assembled.
## Subcontinental note
- Vijayanagara belongs to wider Indian Ocean, Persianate, Kannada, Telugu, and Tamil circuits.
- Political rivalry did not align neatly with religious identity or later nationalism.
## How we know
- Copperplates and stone inscriptions date grants but use formulaic royal praise.
- Archaeology reveals settlement and water systems while leaving political motives uncertain.
- Later texts and Portuguese reports illuminate memory, not eyewitness origins.
## Do not claim
- Do not claim Vijayanagara arose solely to “save Hinduism.”
- Do not claim Harihara and Bukka's conversion story is established fact.
- Do not claim every inscription proves effective territorial control.
- Do not claim warfare prevented diplomacy across confessional lines.
