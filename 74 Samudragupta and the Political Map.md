# Ep 74 — Samudragupta and the Political Map

[Series README](README.md)

## Treatment
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Logline** | A court poem turns campaigns into a ranked political map, revealing both Gupta ambition and the limits of conquest. |
| **Years** | c. 335–375 CE |
| **Before–after** | Chandragupta I’s core realm → a wider hierarchy of tribute, reinstatement, frontier diplomacy, and direct rule |

## Before
The Guptas held a middle-Ganga base amid Nagas, southern kingdoms, forest chiefs, frontier states, and mobile commercial networks.

## The turn
Harishena’s pillar praise classifies rulers by how Samudragupta claims to have defeated, restored, uprooted, or received them.

## After
Successors inherit prestige and resources, but the inscription’s categories never become a uniformly administered subcontinent.

## Act I — Writing over Ashoka
Open on the reused pillar: Mauryan dhamma, later Gupta eulogy, and modern nationalism occupy one object without meaning the same thing.

## Act II — A poem as map
Animate Harishena’s lists by category, not as a single solid-colored empire stretching wherever an army marched.

## Act III — The southern expedition
Trace coastal and inland routes while asking how supplies, diplomacy, and reinstated kings constrained conquest.

## Act IV — King, poet, performer
Coins show Samudragupta sacrificing and playing a lute; these are designed royal personas, not documentary snapshots.

## Act V — Beyond the court
Return to cultivators, artisans, forest communities, women, monasteries, and merchants largely absent from triumphal prose.

## Subcontinental note
Sri Lanka’s Meghavarna sought a Bodh Gaya monastery through diplomacy; that connection did not make the island a Gupta dependency.

## How we know
The Allahabad prashasti, coins, seals, later genealogies, and scattered external notices survive; campaign order and territorial control remain debated.

## Do not claim
- Do not convert every named ruler into a directly governed Gupta province.
- Do not call Samudragupta the “Napoleon of India” without critiquing the colonial analogy.
- Do not treat Harishena’s praise as neutral reportage.
- Do not infer social harmony from elite Sanskrit production.
- Do not erase forest polities by translating *atavika* peoples as empty wilderness.

## Figures, sites, and works
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Samudragupta | Ruler represented through campaigns, ritual, and cultural accomplishment |
| Harishena | Court officer and authorial voice of the prashasti |
| Allahabad pillar prashasti | Main political narrative and a highly interested source |
| Meghavarna | Sri Lankan king linked to Bodh Gaya diplomacy |
| Eran | Site with Gupta-era inscriptions and material remains |
| Lyrist coin | Crafted image of royal refinement and masculinity |

## Locations and visual evidence
Use pillar close-ups, route animation, coin dies, Eran remains, and Bodh Gaya; distinguish attested names, inferred routes, and speculative borders graphically.
